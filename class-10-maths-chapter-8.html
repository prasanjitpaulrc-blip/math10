Exercise 8.1

1. In \(\triangle ABC\), right-angled at \(B,\) \(AB = 24\; cm,\) \(BC = 7\; cm\). Determine:
\(\quad(i)\) \(sin \;A, \;cos \;A\)
\(\quad(ii) \;sin \;C, \;cos \;C\)

Solution:

\( Given,\\ \qquad \triangle ABC \;is \;right-angled \;at \; B,\\ \qquad AB=24\;cm, \;and \;\; BC=7\;cm. \)


\(\;\; By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem, \)

\( \qquad AC^2=AB^2+BC^2 \)

\( \qquad AC^2=24^2+7^2 \)

\( \qquad AC^2=576+49 \)

\( \qquad AC^2=625 \)

\( \qquad AC^2=25^2 \)

\( \quad\therefore AC=25\;cm \)


\( \;(i)\;For\;\angle A, \)

\( \qquad\quad sin \;A =\dfrac{BC}{AC} =\dfrac{7}{25} \)


\( \qquad\quad cos \;A =\dfrac{AB}{AC} =\dfrac{24}{25} \)


\( \;(ii)\;For\;\angle C, \)

\( \qquad\quad sin \;C =\dfrac{AB}{AC} =\dfrac{24}{25} \)


\( \qquad\quad cos \;C =\dfrac{BC}{AC} =\dfrac{7}{25} \)

2. In \(Fig. 8.13,\) find \(tan P - cot R\).

Solution:

\( Given,\\ \qquad \triangle PQR \;is \;right-angled \;at \;Q,\\ \qquad PQ=12\;cm,\;and\; PR=13\;cm. \)


\(\;\; By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem, \)

\( \qquad\;\; PR^2=PQ^2+QR^2 \)

\( \qquad\Rightarrow 13^2=12^2+QR^2 \)

\( \qquad\Rightarrow 169=144+QR^2 \)

\( \qquad\Rightarrow 169-144=QR^2 \)

\( \qquad\Rightarrow QR^2=25 \)

\( \qquad\Rightarrow QR^2=5^2 \)

\( \quad\therefore QR=5\;cm \)

\(\qquad\; tan \;P =\dfrac{QR}{PQ} =\dfrac{5}{12} \)


\(\qquad\; cot \;R =\dfrac{QR}{PQ} =\dfrac{5}{12} \)


\(\;\; \therefore \tan P-\cot R =\dfrac{5}{12}-\dfrac{5}{12} =0 \)

3. If \(sin \;A = \dfrac{3}{4}\), calculate \(cos \;A\) and \(tan \;A\).

Solution:

\(\quad Given,\;\sin A=\dfrac{3}{4}\)

\(\quad\)Let \( \triangle ABC \) be a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(B\).

\(\qquad \sin A=\dfrac{BC}{AC}=\dfrac{3}{4}\)

\(\quad\therefore BC=3k,\;AC=4k\)


\(\qquad By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem,\)

\(\qquad\qquad AC^2=AB^2+BC^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow (4k)^2=AB^2+(3k)^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 16k^2=AB^2+9k^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 16k^2-9k^2=AB^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow AB^2=7k^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow AB=\sqrt7\,k\)


\(\quad\therefore cos\; A=\dfrac{AB}{AC} =\dfrac{\sqrt7\,k}{4k} =\dfrac{\sqrt7}{4}\)


\(\quad tan \;A=\dfrac{BC}{AB} =\dfrac{3k}{\sqrt7\,k} =\dfrac{3}{\sqrt7} \)

4. Given \(15 \;cot \;A = 8,\) find \(sin \;A\) and \(sec \;A\).

Solution:

\(\quad Given,\;15\cot A=8\)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \cot A=\dfrac{8}{15}\)

\(\quad\)Let \( \triangle ABC \) be a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(B\).

\(\qquad \cot A=\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{8}{15}\)

\(\quad\therefore AB=8k,\;BC=15k\)


\(\qquad By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem,\)

\(\qquad AC^2=AB^2+BC^2\)

\(\qquad AC^2 =(8k)^2+(15k)^2\)

\(\qquad AC^2 =64k^2+225k^2\)

\(\qquad AC^2 =289k^2\)

\(\qquad AC^2 =(17k)^2\)

\(\qquad\therefore AC=17k\)


\(\quad\therefore \sin A=\dfrac{BC}{AC} =\dfrac{15k}{17k} =\dfrac{15}{17}\)


\(\quad\quad \sec A=\dfrac{AC}{AB} =\dfrac{17k}{8k} =\dfrac{17}{8}\)

5. Given \(sec \;\theta = \dfrac{13}{12}\), calculate all other trigonometric ratios.

Solution:

\(\quad Given,\;\sec\theta=\dfrac{13}{12}\)

\(\quad\)Let \( \triangle ABC \) be a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(B\).

\(\qquad \sec A=\dfrac{AC}{AB}=\dfrac{13}{12}\)

\(\quad\therefore AC=13k,\;AB=12k\)


\(\quad\; By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem,\)

\(\qquad\quad AC^2=AB^2+BC^2\)

\(\quad\;\Rightarrow (13k)^2=(12k)^2+BC^2\)

\(\quad\;\Rightarrow 169k^2=144k^2+BC^2\)

\(\quad\;\Rightarrow 169k^2-144k^2=BC^2\)

\(\quad\;\Rightarrow BC^2=25k^2\)

\(\quad\;\Rightarrow BC^2=(5k)^2\)

\(\qquad\therefore BC=5k\)


\(\quad\therefore sin\;\theta=\dfrac{BC}{AC} =\dfrac{5k}{13k} =\dfrac{5}{13}\)


\(\quad\quad cos\;\theta=\dfrac{AB}{AC} =\dfrac{12k}{13k} =\dfrac{12}{13}\)


\(\quad\quad tan\;\theta=\dfrac{BC}{AB} =\dfrac{5k}{12k} =\dfrac{5}{12}\)


\(\quad\quad cot\;\theta=\dfrac{AB}{BC} =\dfrac{12k}{5k} =\dfrac{12}{5}\)


\(\quad\quad cosec\;\theta=\dfrac{AC}{BC} =\dfrac{13k}{5k} =\dfrac{13}{5}\)

Solution:

\(\qquad Let\;\triangle ABC\;is\;a\;right\) \(angled\) \(triangle\) \(where\) \(\angle A\) \(and\) \(\angle B\) \(are\) \(acute\) \(angles,\)

\(\qquad\quad\therefore angle C=90^\circ\)

\(\quad Given,\)

\(\qquad\quad\cos A=\cos B \)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow\dfrac{AC}{AB}=\dfrac{BC}{AB}\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow AC=BC\)

\(\qquad\therefore\angle A=\angle B\)

\(\qquad\qquad\text{(Angles opposite to equal sides are equal)}\)

7. If \(cot \;\theta = \dfrac{7}{8}\), evaluate:

\(\quad(i) \;\dfrac{(1+\sin\theta)(1-\sin\theta)}{(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)}\)
\(\quad(ii) \;\cot^2\theta\)

Solution:

\(\quad Given,\;cot\;\theta=\dfrac{7}{8}\)

\(\quad\)Let \( \triangle ABC \) be a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(B\).

\(\qquad\;\; cot\theta=\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{7}{8}\)

\(\quad\therefore AB=7k,\;BC=8k\)


\(\quad\; By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem,\)

\(\qquad AC^2=AB^2+BC^2\)

\(\qquad\qquad =(7k)^2+(8k)^2\)

\(\qquad\qquad =49k^2+64k^2\)

\(\qquad\qquad =113k^2\)

\(\qquad AC=\sqrt{113}\,k\)


\(\quad\therefore\; \sin\theta=\dfrac{BC}{AC} =\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{113}}\)

\(\;and\; \cos\theta=\dfrac{AB}{AC} =\dfrac{7}{\sqrt{113}}\)


\(\;(i)\; \dfrac{(1+\sin\theta)(1-\sin\theta)} {(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)} \)


\(\qquad =\dfrac{1-\sin^2\theta}{1-\cos^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad =\dfrac{1-\left(\dfrac{8}{\sqrt{113}}\right)^2}{1-\left(\dfrac{7}{\sqrt{113}}\right)^2} =\dfrac{1-\left(\dfrac{64}{113}\right)}{1-\left(\dfrac{49}{113}\right)} \)


\(\qquad =\dfrac{\dfrac{113-64}{113}}{\dfrac{113-49}{113}} =\dfrac{\dfrac{49}{113}}{\dfrac{64}{113}} \)


\(\qquad =\dfrac{49}{113}\times\dfrac{113}{64} =\dfrac{49}{64} \)


\(\;(ii)\; \cot^2\theta =\left(\dfrac{7}{8}\right)^2 =\dfrac{49}{64} \)

8. If \(3 \;cot \;A = 4,\) check whether \(\dfrac{1-\tan^2 A}{1+\tan^2 A} = \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A\) or \(not\).

Solution:

\(\quad Given,\;3\cot A=4\)

\(\qquad\therefore \cot A=\dfrac{4}{3}\)

\(\quad\)Let \( \triangle ABC \) be a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(B\).

\(\qquad\; \cot A=\dfrac{AB}{BC}=\dfrac{4}{3}\)

\(\quad\;\;\therefore AB=4k,\;BC=3k\)


\(\quad\;\; By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem,\)

\(\qquad\; AC^2=AB^2+BC^2\)

\(\qquad\; AC^2 =(4k)^2+(3k)^2\)

\(\qquad\; AC^2=16k^2+9k^2\)

\(\qquad\; AC^2=25k^2\)

\(\quad\;\;\therefore AC=5k\)


\(\quad From\;\triangle ABC,\)

\(\quad \sin A=\dfrac35,\; \cos A=\dfrac45,\; \tan A=\dfrac34\)

\(\;Now,\)

\(\quad LHS =\dfrac{1-\tan^2A}{1+\tan^2A} =\dfrac{1-\left(\dfrac34\right)^2} {1+\left(\dfrac34\right)^2} \)

\(\qquad\quad\;\; =\dfrac{1-\dfrac{9}{16}} {1+\dfrac{9}{16}} =\dfrac{\dfrac{16-9}{16}} {\dfrac{16+9}{16}} =\dfrac{\dfrac{7}{16}} {\dfrac{25}{16}} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\;\; =\dfrac{7}{16}\times\dfrac{16}{25} =\dfrac{7}{25} \)

\(\;\; RHS =\cos^2A-\sin^2A \)

\(\qquad\;\; =\left(\dfrac45\right)^2- \left(\dfrac35\right)^2 =\dfrac{16}{25}-\dfrac{9}{25} =\dfrac{7}{25} \)

\(\qquad\because LHS=RHS\)

\(\qquad\therefore \dfrac{1-\tan^2 A}{1+\tan^2 A} = \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A\) .

9. In triangle \(ABC,\) right-angled at \(B,\) if \(\tan A = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\), find the value of:
\(\;(i) \;sin \;A \;cos \;C + cos \;A \;sin \;C\)
\(\;(ii) \;cos \;A \;cos\; C - sin \;A \;sin\; C\)

Solution:

\(\quad Given,\;\tan A=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\)

\(\quad\)Let \( \triangle ABC \) be a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(B\).

\(\qquad \tan A=\dfrac{BC}{AB}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\)

\(\qquad \therefore BC=1k,\;AB=\sqrt3\,k\)


\(\qquad By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem,\)

\(\qquad AC^2=AB^2+BC^2\)

\(\qquad AC^2=(\sqrt3\,k)^2+(1k)^2\)

\(\qquad AC^2=3k^2+k^2=4k^2\)

\(\qquad\therefore AC=2k\)


\(\; \therefore\) \( \sin A=\dfrac{BC}{AC}=\dfrac{1}{2},\; \cos A=\dfrac{AB}{AC}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2} \)
\(\;\; \sin C=\dfrac{AB}{AC}=\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\; \cos C=\dfrac{BC}{AC}=\dfrac{1}{2} \)

\(\qquad Now,\)

\(\quad (i)\; \sin A\cos C+\cos A\sin C \)

\(\qquad =\left(\dfrac12\right)\left(\dfrac12\right) +\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) \)

\(\qquad =\dfrac14+\dfrac34 \)

\(\qquad\quad =\dfrac44=1 \)


\(\quad (ii)\; \cos A\cos C-\sin A\sin C \)

\(\qquad =\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)\left(\dfrac12\right) -\left(\dfrac12\right)\left(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) \)

\(\qquad =\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4} =0 \)

10. In \(\triangle PQR\), right-angled at \(Q,\) \(PR + QR = 25 \;cm\) and \(PQ = 5 \;cm\). Determine the values of \(sin \;P,\; cos\; P\) and \(tan \;P\).

Solution:

\(\; Given,\;PQ=5\;cm,\;PR+QR=25\;cm\)

\(\qquad Let \;QR=x\;cm\).

\(\qquad\therefore PR=(25-x)\;cm\)


\(\quad\; By\;Pythagoras\;Theorem,\)

\(\qquad\quad PR^2=PQ^2+QR^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow (25-x)^2=5^2+x^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 625-50x+x^2=25+x^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 625-50x=25\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 625-25=50x\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 600=50x\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow \dfrac{600}{50}=x\)

\(\qquad\quad x=12\)

\(\qquad\therefore QR=12\;cm\)

\(\qquad PR=25-12=13\;cm\)


\(\qquad\therefore \sin P=\dfrac{QR}{PR} =\dfrac{12}{13}\)


\(\qquad\quad \cos P=\dfrac{PQ}{PR} =\dfrac{5}{13}\)


\(\qquad\quad \tan P=\dfrac{QR}{PQ} =\dfrac{12}{5}\)

11. State whether the following are true or false. Justify your answer.
\(\;\;(i)\) The value of \(tan \;A\) is always less than \(1\).
\(\;\;(ii)\) \(sec \;A = \dfrac{12}{5}\) for some value of angle \(A\).
\(\;\;(iii)\) \(cos \;A\) is the abbreviation used for the \(cosecant\) of angle \(A\).
\(\;\;(iv) \;\;cot \;A\) is the product of \(cot\) and \(A\).
\(\;\;(v)\) \(sin \;\theta = \dfrac{4}{3}\) for some angle \(\theta\).

Solution:

\(\quad (i)\) The value of \( \tan A \) is always less than \(1\) is false.

\(\quad \;The \;value \;of \tan A \;may \;be \;less \;than,\) \(equal\) \(to\) \(or\) \(greater\) \(than\) \(1\). \(For\) \(example,\) \( \tan 60^\circ=\sqrt3>1. \)

\(\quad (ii)\) \( \sec A=\dfrac{12}{5} \) for some value of angle \(A\) is true.

\(\qquad Since\) \( \sec A=\dfrac{1}{\cos A}, \) \(we\) \(get\) \( \cos A=\dfrac{5}{12}, \) \(which\) \(is\) \(possible\) \(because\) \(0 < \dfrac{5}{12} <1 \).

\(\quad (iii)\) \( \cos A \) is the abbreviation used for the cosecant of angle \(A\) is false.

\(\quad\)\(\cos A \;denotes \;the \;cosine \;of \;angleA,\) \(whereas\) \(the\) \(abbreviation\) \(for\) \(cosecant\) \(is\) \(\cosec A\).

\(\quad (iv)\) \( \cot A \) is the product of \(cot\) and \(A\) is false.

\(\quad\)\(\cot A \;is \;the \;cotangent \;of \;angle A\). \(It\) \(is\) \(a\) \(trigonometric\) \(ratio,\) \(not\) \(the\) \(product\) \(of\) \(cot\) \(and\) \(A\).

\(\quad (v)\) \( \sin\theta=\dfrac{4}{3} \) for some angle \(\theta\) is false.

\(\qquad The \;value \;of \sin\theta \;always \;lies \;between\) \(-1\) \(and\) \(1\). \(Since\) \( \dfrac{4}{3}>1, \) \(it\) \(is\) \(not\) \(possible\).

\(\;\;(a)\;\dfrac53\;\;\;(b)\;\dfrac35\;\;\;(c)\;\dfrac45\;\;\;(d)\;\dfrac54\)

Solution:\((c)\) \(\dfrac45\) is the correct option.



Explanation:

\(\quad\)Let \( \triangle ABC \) be a right-angled triangle, right-angled at \(B\).

\(\qquad \sin A=\dfrac{BC}{AC}=\dfrac35\)

\(\qquad \therefore BC=3k,\;AC=5k\)

\(\qquad AC^2=AB^2+BC^2\)

\(\qquad (5k)^2=AB^2+(3k)^2\)

\(\qquad 25k^2=AB^2+9k^2\)

\(\qquad AB^2=16k^2\)

\(\qquad \therefore AB=4k\)


\(\qquad \cos A=\dfrac{AB}{AC} =\dfrac{4k}{5k} =\dfrac45\)

5. Prove the following identities, where the angles involved are acute angles for which the expressions are defined.
\((i) (\cosec \theta - \cot \theta)^2 = \dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{1+\cos\theta}\)

\((ii) \dfrac{\cos A}{1+\sin A} + \dfrac{1+\sin A}{\cos A} = 2\sec A\)


\((iii) \frac{\tan\theta}{1-\cot\theta} + \frac{\cot\theta}{1-\tan\theta} = 1 + \sec\theta \cosec\theta\)


\((iv) \dfrac{1+\sec A}{\sec A} = \dfrac{\sin^2 A}{1-\cos A}\)


\((v) \frac{\cos A - \sin A + 1}{\cos A + \sin A - 1} = \cosec A + \cot A\), using the identity \(\cosec^2 A = 1 + \cot^2 A\)

\((ix) (\cosec A - \sin A)(\sec A - \cos A) = \frac{1}{\tan A + \cot A}\)


\((x) \left(\frac{1+\tan^2 A}{1+\cot^2 A}\right) = \left(\frac{1-\tan A}{1-\cot A}\right)^2 = \tan^2 A\)

Solution:

\(\quad (i)\;LHS=\; (\cosec\theta-\cot\theta)^2 \)


\(\qquad = \left( \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta} - \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \right)^2 \)


\(\qquad = \left( \dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \right)^2 \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{(1-\cos\theta)^2}{\sin^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{(1-\cos\theta)^2} {1-\cos^2\theta} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad[\because\sin^2 A=1-\cos^2 A]\)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{(1-\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)} {1^2-\cos^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{(1-\cos\theta)^2} {(1-\cos\theta)(1+\cos\theta)} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\because a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)]\)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{1+\cos\theta}=RHS \)


\(\;\therefore (\cosec\theta-\cot\theta)^2 = \dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{1+\cos\theta} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence, \;proved.\)



\(\quad (ii)\;LHS=\; \dfrac{\cos A}{1+\sin A} + \dfrac{1+\sin A}{\cos A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cos^2A+(1+\sin A)^2} {(1+\sin A)\cos A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cos^2A+(1^2+2\sin A+\sin^2A)} {(1+\sin A)\cos A} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\because(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2]\)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sin^2A+\cos^2A+1+2\sin A} {(1+\sin A)\cos A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1+1+2\sin A} {(1+\sin A)\cos A} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\because\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1]\)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{2+2\sin A} {(1+\sin A)\cos A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{2(1+\sin A)} {(1+\sin A)\cos A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{2\times1}{\cos A} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\left[\because \dfrac{1}{cos\theta}=\sec \theta\right]\)

\(\qquad =2\sec A=RHS \)


\(\;\therefore\; \dfrac{\cos A}{1+\sin A} + \dfrac{1+\sin A}{\cos A} = 2\sec A \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence, \;proved.\)



\(\quad (iii)\;LHS=\; \dfrac{\tan\theta}{1-\cot\theta} + \dfrac{\cot\theta}{1-\tan\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}} {1-\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}} + \dfrac{\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}} {1-\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}} {\dfrac{\sin\theta-\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}} + \dfrac{\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}} {\dfrac{\cos\theta-\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sin^2\theta} {\cos\theta(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)} + \dfrac{\cos^2\theta} {\sin\theta(\cos\theta-\sin\theta)} \)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sin^2\theta} {\cos\theta(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)} - \dfrac{\cos^2\theta} {\sin\theta(\sin\theta-\cos\theta)} \)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{ \sin^3\theta-\cos^3\theta } { \sin\theta\cos\theta(\sin\theta-\cos\theta) } \)


\(\quad = \dfrac{ (\sin\theta-\cos\theta) (\sin^2\theta+\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta) } { \sin\theta\cos\theta(\sin\theta-\cos\theta) } \)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad [\because a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)]\)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{ \sin^2\theta+\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta } { \sin\theta\cos\theta } \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{ 1+\sin\theta\cos\theta } { \sin\theta\cos\theta } \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{ 1 } { \sin\theta\cos\theta }+\dfrac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\sin\theta\cos\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}+1 \)


\(\qquad = \sec\theta\cosec\theta+1 \)


\(\qquad =1+\sec\theta\cosec\theta=RHS \)


\(\therefore\dfrac{\tan\theta}{1-\cot\theta} + \dfrac{\cot\theta}{1-\tan\theta} =1+\sec\theta\cosec\theta \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence, \;proved.\)



\(\quad (iv)\;LHS=\; \dfrac{1+\sec A}{\sec A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1+\dfrac{1}{\cos A}} {\dfrac{1}{\cos A}} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\dfrac{cosA +1}{\cos A}} {\dfrac{1}{\cos A}} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cos A+1}{\cos A} \times \dfrac{\cos A}{1} \)


\(\qquad =1+\cos A \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad denominator\;by\;(1-cosA)]\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad denominator\;by\;sinA]\)

\((xii) \;\tan^2 A - \tan^2 B = \frac{\sin^2A- \sin^2 B}{\cos^2A \cos^2B}\)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{\left(\dfrac{1}{\sin A}-1\right)\cos A} {\left(\dfrac{1}{\sin A}+1\right)\cos A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{\sin A}-1} {\dfrac{1}{\sin A}+1} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cosec A-1}{\cosec A+1} =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (iii)\;LHS\;:\)

\(\qquad\qquad \dfrac{\cos A}{1-\tan A} -\dfrac{\sin^2A}{\cos A-\sin A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cos A} {1-\dfrac{\sin A}{\cos A}} -\dfrac{\sin^2A}{\cos A-\sin A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cos A} {\dfrac{\cos A-\sin A}{\cos A}} -\dfrac{\sin^2A}{\cos A-\sin A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cos^2A}{\cos A-\sin A} -\dfrac{\sin^2A}{\cos A-\sin A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\cos^2A-\sin^2A} {\cos A-\sin A} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{(\cos A-\sin A)(\cos A+\sin A)} {\cos A-\sin A} \)


\(\qquad = \cos A+\sin A \)


\(\qquad =\sin A+\cos A=RHS \)


\(\qquad\therefore\; \dfrac{\cos A}{1-\tan A} -\dfrac{\sin^2A}{\cos A-\sin A} =\sin A+\cos A \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (iv)\;LHS\;= \dfrac{\cosec\theta+\cot\theta} {\cosec\theta-\cot\theta} \)


\(\quad = \dfrac{(\cosec\theta+\cot\theta)} {(\cosec\theta-\cot\theta)} \times \dfrac{(\cosec\theta+\cot\theta)} {(\cosec\theta+\cot\theta)} \)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{(\cosec\theta+\cot\theta)^2} {\cosec^2\theta-\cot^2\theta} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\because \cosec^2\theta-\cot^2\theta=1]\)

\(\qquad = (\cosec\theta+\cot\theta)^2 \)

\(\qquad = \cosec^2\theta+\cot^2\theta +2\cosec\theta\cot\theta \)

\(\quad = (1+\cot^2\theta)+\cot^2\theta +2\cosec\theta\cot\theta \)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\because \cosec^2\theta=1+\cot^2\theta]\)
\(\quad = 1+2\cot^2\theta +2\cosec\theta\cot\theta =RHS \)

\(\;\therefore\; \dfrac{\cosec\theta+\cot\theta} {\cosec\theta-\cot\theta} = 1+2\cot^2\theta +2\cosec\theta\cot\theta \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (v)\;LHS\; = \sqrt{\dfrac{1+\cos\beta}{1-\cos\beta}} \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{\dfrac{(1+\cos\beta)(1+\cos\beta)}{(1-\cos\beta)(1+\cos\beta)}} \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{(1+\cos\beta)^2} {1-\cos^2\beta} } \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{(1+\cos\beta)^2} {\sin^2\beta} } \)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad[\because \sin^2 \theta +\cos^2 \theta=1 \Rightarrow (1-\cos^2 \theta)=\sin^2 \theta]\)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{1+\cos\beta}{\sin\beta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1}{\sin\beta} + \dfrac{\cos\beta}{\sin\beta} \)


\(\qquad = \cosec\beta+\cot\beta =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (vi)\;LHS:\;\)
\( \qquad\qquad \sqrt{\dfrac{1+\cos\theta}{1-\cos\theta}} + \sqrt{\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{1+\cos\theta}} \)


\( = \sqrt{\dfrac{(1+\cos\theta)(1+\cos\theta)}{(1-\cos\theta)(1+\cos\theta)}} + \sqrt{\dfrac{(1-\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)}{(1+\cos\theta)(1-\cos\theta)}} \)

\(\qquad = \sqrt{\dfrac{(1+\cos\theta)^2}{1-\cos^2\theta}} + \sqrt{\dfrac{(1-\cos\theta)^2}{1-\cos^2\theta}} \)

\(\qquad = \sqrt{\dfrac{(1+\cos\theta)^2}{\sin^2\theta}} + \sqrt{\dfrac{(1-\cos\theta)^2}{\sin^2\theta}} \)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} + \dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1+\cos\theta+1-\cos\theta} {\sin\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{2}{\sin\theta} \)


\(\qquad = 2\cosec\theta =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (vii)\;LHS\; = \sqrt{\dfrac{\sec\theta+1}{\sec\theta-1}} \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{\dfrac1{\cos\theta}+1} {\dfrac1{\cos\theta}-1} } \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{\dfrac{1+\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}} {\dfrac{1-\cos\theta}{\cos\theta}} } \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{1+\cos\theta} {1-\cos\theta} } \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{(1+\cos\theta)(1+\cos\theta)} {(1-\cos\theta)(1+\cos\theta)} } \)


\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{(1+\cos\theta)^2} {1-\cos^2\theta} } \)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad[\because \sin^2 \theta +\cos^2 \theta=1 \Rightarrow (1-\cos^2 \theta)=\sin^2 \theta]\)

\(\qquad = \sqrt{ \dfrac{(1+\cos\theta)^2} {\sin^2 \theta} } \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac1{\sin\theta} + \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \cosec\theta+\cot\theta =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (viii)\;LHS\; = \dfrac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos^2\theta} + \dfrac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sin^4\theta+\cos^4\theta} {\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{(\sin^2\theta)^2+(\cos^2\theta)^2} {\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\because a^2+b^2=(a+b)^2-2ab]\)

\(\quad = \dfrac{(\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta)^2 -2\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} {\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1^2-2\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} {\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1} {\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta}-\dfrac{2\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} {\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta}-2 =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (ix):\;LHS\;\)

\( \qquad \sin\theta\cos(90^\circ-\theta) + \cos\theta\sin(90^\circ-\theta) \)

\(\qquad = \sin\theta\sin\theta + \cos\theta\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad = \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta \)

\(\qquad = 1=RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (x)\;LHS\; = \dfrac{1}{\sec\theta-\tan\theta} - \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta} \)


\(\quad = \dfrac{1\times(\sec\theta+\tan\theta)}{(\sec\theta-\tan\theta)(\sec\theta+\tan\theta)} - \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta} \)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sec\theta+\tan\theta}{\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta}-\sec\theta \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sec\theta+\tan\theta}{1}-\sec\theta \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad [\because \sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta=1]\)

\(\qquad = \sec\theta+\tan\theta-\sec\theta \)


\(\quad = \sec\theta-\dfrac{(\sec\theta-\tan\theta)(\sec\theta+\tan\theta)}{(\sec\theta+\tan\theta)} \)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta} - \dfrac{\sec^2\theta-\tan^2\theta}{\sec\theta+\tan\theta} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta} - \dfrac{1}{\sec\theta+\tan\theta} =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (xi)\;LHS\; = \sec^2\alpha\cosec^2\alpha \)

\(\qquad = (1+\tan^2\alpha)(1+\cot^2\alpha) \)

\(\quad = 1+\cot^2\alpha+\tan^2\alpha +\tan^2\alpha\cot^2\alpha \)

\(\quad = 1+\tan^2\alpha+\cot^2\alpha +\tan^2\alpha\dfrac{1}{tan^2\alpha} \)

\(\qquad = 1+\tan^2\alpha+\cot^2\alpha+1 \)

\(\qquad = \tan^2\alpha+\cot^2\alpha+2 =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)



\(\quad (xii)\;LHS\; = \tan^2A-\tan^2B \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sin^2A}{\cos^2A} - \dfrac{\sin^2B}{\cos^2B} \)


\(\qquad = \dfrac{ \sin^2A\cos^2B-\sin^2B\cos^2A } {\cos^2A\cos^2B} \)


\(\; = \dfrac{ \sin^2A(1-\sin^2B) - \sin^2B(1-\sin^2A) } {\cos^2A\cos^2B} \)

\(\quad = \dfrac{ \sin^2A-\sin^2A\sin^2B - \sin^2B+\sin^2A\sin^2B } {\cos^2A\cos^2B} \)

\(\qquad = \dfrac{\sin^2A-\sin^2B} {\cos^2A\cos^2B} \)


\(\qquad =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)

8. \((i)\) If \(\cos\theta+\sin\theta=\sqrt{2}\cos\theta\), show that \(\cos\theta-\sin\theta=\sqrt{2}\sin\theta\).

\((ii)\) If \(\sin\theta+\sin^2\theta=1\), prove that \(\cos^2\theta+\cos^4\theta=1\).

Solution:

\(\quad (i)\;Given,\)

\(\qquad\quad \cos\theta+\sin\theta=\sqrt2\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad\quad \sin\theta=\sqrt2\cos\theta-\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad \Rightarrow\; \sin\theta=(\sqrt2-1)\cos\theta \quad\ldots(i) \)

\(\quad LHS= \cos\theta-\sin\theta \)

\(\qquad = \cos\theta-(\sqrt2-1)\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad = \cos\theta-\sqrt2\cos\theta+\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad = 2\cos\theta-\sqrt2\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad = (2-\sqrt2)\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad = \sqrt2(\sqrt2-1)\cos\theta \)

\(\qquad = \sqrt2\sin\theta \qquad\text{[Using (i)]} \)

\(\qquad =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence \;proved. \)



\(\quad (ii)\;Given,\)

\(\qquad\quad \sin\theta+\sin^2\theta=1 \)

\(\qquad \Rightarrow\; \sin\theta=1-\sin^2\theta \)

\(\qquad \Rightarrow\; \sin\theta=\cos^2\theta \qquad\ldots(i) \)


\(\quad LHS= \cos^2\theta+\cos^4\theta \)

\(\qquad = \cos^2\theta+(\cos^2\theta)^2 \)

\(\qquad = \sin\theta+\sin^2\theta \qquad\text{[Using (i)]} \)

\(\qquad =1 \qquad\qquad [Given] \)

\(\qquad =RHS \)


\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved. \)

NCERT Class 10 Math Chapter 8 – Introduction to Trigonometry FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Trigonometry.

What are the six trigonometric ratios?

sin, cos, tan, cosec, sec, cot. Defined as ratios of sides in a right‑angled triangle.

What is the value of sin 45°?

sin 45° = \(1/\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2}/2\).

What is the identity sin² θ + cos² θ = ?

It equals 1. This is the fundamental trigonometric identity.

How to remember the table of trigonometric ratios?

Use the sequence 0,1,2,3,4 divided by 4, square root for sin; reverse for cos; tan = sin/cos.

What is the complementary angle relation?

sin(90°−θ) = cos θ, cos(90°−θ) = sin θ, tan(90°−θ) = cot θ, etc.

What is the maximum value of sin θ?

1, attained at θ = 90°.

How to prove trigonometric identities?

Start with one side, convert to sin and cos if needed, and simplify using algebraic identities.

What is sec² A − tan² A?

It equals 1, derived from sin²A+cos²A=1 dividing by cos²A.

Can sin θ be greater than 1?

No, because sin is opposite/hypotenuse, and hypotenuse is the longest side.

What is the value of tan 90°?

Undefined (approaches infinity).
