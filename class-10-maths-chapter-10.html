Circles in Class 10 focuses on the tangent to a circle and its properties. You will learn that a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, that tangents drawn from an external point are equal in length, and how to apply these properties to solve geometric problems involving circles, secants, and tangents.

This chapter is short but highly conceptual and scoring. Mastering the properties of tangents opens the door to solving a variety of problems, from finding lengths to proving geometric relationships. It also lays the groundwork for more advanced geometry in higher classes.

Exercise 10.1

1. How many tangents can a circle have?

Solution: A circle can have infinitely many tangents — one at each point on its circumference.

2. Fill in the blanks :
\((i)\) A tangent to a circle intersects it in ______ point(s).
\((ii)\) A line intersecting a circle in two points is called a ______.
\((iii)\) A circle can have ______ parallel tangents at the most.
\((iv)\) The common point of a tangent to a circle and the circle is called ______.

Solution: (i) one, (ii) secant, (iii) two, (iv) point of contact.

3. A tangent \(PQ\) at a point \(P\) of a circle of radius \(5\; cm\) meets a line through the centre O at a point \(Q\) so that \(OQ = 12 \;cm\). Find the length \(PQ\).
\(\;\;(A)\;12\;cm\qquad (B)\;13\;cm\)
\(\;\;(C)\;8.2\;cm\qquad (D)\;\sqrt{119}\;cm\)

Solution: Since the tangent is perpendicular to the radius OP at the point of contact, \(\triangle OPQ\) is right‑angled at P. OP = 5 cm, OQ = 12 cm. By Pythagoras theorem, \(PQ = \sqrt{OQ^2 - OP^2} = \sqrt{12^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144 - 25} = \sqrt{119}\) cm.

Exercise 10.2

1. From a point \(Q,\) the length of the tangent to a circle is \(24 \;cm\) and the distance of \(Q\) from the centre is \(25\; cm\). Find the radius of the circle
\(\quad (A) \;7\;cm\) \(\qquad (B) \;12\;cm\)
\(\quad (C) \;15\;cm\) \(\quad\; (D) \;24.5\;cm\).

Solution: In right triangle formed by radius \(r\), tangent, and OQ, \(OQ^2 = r^2 + \text{tangent}^2\). So \(25^2 = r^2 + 24^2 \;\;\Rightarrow 625 = r^2 + 576 \;\;\Rightarrow r^2 = 49 \;\;\Rightarrow r = 7\) cm.

2. In \(Fig.\; 10.15,\) \(\;TP\) and \(TQ\) are two tangents to a circle with centre O such that \(\angle POQ = 110°\). Then \(\angle PTQ\) is equal to
\(\quad (A) \;60^\circ\) \(\qquad (B) \;70^\circ\)
\(\quad (C) \;80^\circ\) \(\qquad (D) \;90^\circ\)

Solution: OP ⟂ TP, OQ ⟂ TQ, so ∠OPT = ∠OQT = 90°. In quadrilateral OPTQ, sum of angles = 360°. ∠PTQ = 360° − 90° − 90° − 110° = 70°. Answer (B).

3. If tangents \(PA\) and \(PB\) from a point \(P\) to a circle with centre \(O\) are inclined to each other at angle of \(80^\circ,\) then \(\angle POA\) is equal to
\(\quad (A)\; 50^\circ\) \(\qquad (B) \;60^\circ\)
\(\quad (C) \;70^\circ\) \(\qquad (D) \;80^\circ\).

Solution: OP bisects ∠APB, so ∠APO = 40°. In right triangle OAP, ∠POA = 90° − 40° = 50°. Answer (A).

4. Prove that the tangents drawn at the ends of a diameter of a circle are parallel.

Solution: Let AB be a diameter, with tangents at A and B. OA is perpendicular to tangent at A, OB is perpendicular to tangent at B. Since OA and OB are parts of the same straight line (diameter), both tangents are perpendicular to the same line, hence they are parallel.

5. Prove that the perpendicular at the point of contact to the tangent to a circle passes through the centre.

Solution: By definition, the radius drawn to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent. So the line perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact must be along the radius, and thus passes through the centre. (Proof by contradiction also possible.)

6. The length of a tangent from a point \(A\) at distance \(5 \;cm\) from the centre of the circle is \(4\; cm\). Find the radius of the circle.

Solution: \(r = \sqrt{OA^2 - \text{tangent}^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = \sqrt{25 - 16} = 3\) cm.

7. Two concentric circles are of radii \(5 \;cm\) and \(3\; cm\). Find the length of the chord of the larger circle which touches the smaller circle.

Solution: Let chord AB of larger circle touch smaller circle at C. OC ⟂ AB, OC = 3 cm (radius of smaller). OA = 5 cm. In right triangle OCA, AC = \(\sqrt{5^2 - 3^2} = 4\) cm. Since perpendicular from centre bisects chord, AB = 2 × 4 = 8 cm.

8. A quadrilateral \(ABCD\) is drawn to circumscribe a circle \((see \;Fig.\; 10.16)\). Prove that \(AB + CD = AD + BC\).

Solution: Let the points of tangency on AB, BC, CD, DA be P, Q, R, S respectively. Then AP = AS, BP = BQ, CQ = CR, DR = DS. Adding: (AP+BP) + (CR+DR) = (AS+DS) + (BQ+CQ) → AB + CD = AD + BC.

9. In \(Fig. \;10.17,\) \(\;XY\) and \(X'Y'\) are two parallel tangents to a circle with centre \(O\) and another tangent \(AB\) with point of contact \(C\) intersecting \(XY\) at \(A\) and \(X'Y'\) at \(B\). Prove that \(\angle AOB = 90^\circ\).

Solution: Tangents from external point A are equal: AP = AC, and OA bisects ∠PAC. Similarly, OB bisects ∠QBC. Since XY ∥ X′Y′, ∠PAC + ∠QBC = 180° (co‑interior angles). Thus half their sum = 90°. In quadrilateral OAB?, using angle sums we get ∠AOB = 90°. (Standard proof: triangles OAP and OAC are congruent, etc.)

10. Prove that the angle between the two tangents drawn from an external point to a circle is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line-segment joining the points of contact at the centre.

Solution: Let tangents PA, PB from P touch circle at A, B. OA ⟂ PA, OB ⟂ PB. In quadrilateral OAPB, ∠APB + ∠AOB + 90° + 90° = 360° → ∠APB + ∠AOB = 180°. Hence supplementary.

11. Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.

Solution: Let parallelogram ABCD circumscribe a circle. Since tangents from a vertex to the circle are equal, we have: from A, AS = AP; from B, BP = BQ; etc. Using properties of parallelogram (AB = CD, BC = AD) and tangent equalities, we get AB = BC, so all sides equal. Hence rhombus.

12. A triangle \(ABC\) is drawn to circumscribe a circle of radius \(4\; cm\) such that the segments \(BD\) and \(DC\) into which \(BC\) is divided by the point of contact \(D\) are of lengths \(8 \;cm\) and \(6 \;cm\) respectively \((see \;Fig. \;10.18)\). Find the sides \(AB\) and \(AC\).

Solution: Let tangents from B: BD = BE = 8 cm, from C: CD = CF = 6 cm, from A: AF = AE = x cm. So AB = x+8, AC = x+6, BC = 8+6=14. Use area = rs. Semi‑perimeter s = (AB+AC+BC)/2 = (x+8 + x+6 + 14)/2 = x+14. Area also = (1/2)×BC×height? Better: Area = rs = 4(x+14). Also using Heron? Or equate area using base and altitude? There's a direct relation: area = √[s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)]. We can set up equation: √[(x+14)(x)(8)(6)] = 4(x+14). Square: 48x(x+14) = 16(x+14)² → 3x = x+14 → 2x=14 → x=7. So AB=15 cm, AC=13 cm.

13. Prove that opposite sides of a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle subtend supplementary angles at the centre of the circle.

Solution: Let quadrilateral ABCD circumscribe circle, touching at P, Q, R, S. By tangent properties, the angles subtended by the sides at the centre can be shown to sum to 180° for opposite sides. (Using that OA, OB etc. bisect angles and sum of angles around centre is 360°.)

NCERT Class 10 Math Chapter 10 – Circles FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Circles.

What is a tangent to a circle?

A line that touches the circle at exactly one point (point of contact). It is perpendicular to the radius at that point.

How many tangents can be drawn from an external point?

Exactly two tangents, and they are equal in length.

What is the angle between radius and tangent?

90° (perpendicular).

What is a secant?

A line that intersects the circle at two distinct points.

What is the maximum number of parallel tangents?

Two (at the ends of a diameter).

How to find the length of a tangent from an external point?

Length = \(\sqrt{d^2 - r^2}\), where d is the distance from the point to the centre, r is the radius.

Why are tangents from an external point equal?

Because the two right triangles formed with radii are congruent (RHS).

What is a concentric circle?

Circles with the same centre but different radii.

How to prove a quadrilateral circumscribing a circle is a rhombus?

If it's a parallelogram, use equal tangents from vertices to show adjacent sides are equal, hence all sides equal.

What is the relationship between the angle between tangents and the central angle?

Angle between two tangents from an external point is supplementary to the angle subtended by the line segment joining the points of contact at the centre.
