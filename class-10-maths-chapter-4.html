Exercise 4.1

1. Check whether the following are quadratic equations:
\((i)\) \((x+1)^2 = 2(x-3)\)
\((ii)\) \(x^2 - 2x = (-2)(3-x)\)
\((iii)\) \((x-2)(x+1) = (x-1)(x+3)\)
\((iv)\) \((x-3)(2x+1) = x(x+5)\)
\((v)\) \((2x-1)(x-3) = (x+5)(x-1)\)
\((vi)\) \(x^2 + 3x + 1 = (x-2)^2\)
\((vii)\) \((x+2)^3 = 2x(x^2-1)\)
\((viii)\) \(x^3 - 4x^2 - x + 1 = (x-2)^3\)

Solution:

\(\quad(i)\)

\(\qquad (x+1)^2=2(x-3)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+2x+1=2x-6\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+2x+1-2x+6=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+7=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(2\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\quad(ii)\)

\(\qquad x^2-2x=(-2)(3-x)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-2x=-6+2x\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-2x+6-2x=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-4x+6=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(2\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\;(iii)\)

\(\;(x-2)(x+1)=(x-1)(x+3)\)

\(\;\Rightarrow x^2+x-2x-2=x^2+3x-x-3\)

\(\;\Rightarrow x^2-x-2=x^2+2x-3\)
\(\;\Rightarrow x^2-x-2-x^2-2x+3=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow -3x+1=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(1\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;not\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\qquad (x-3)(2x+1)=x(x+5)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 2x^2+x-6x-3=x^2+5x\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 2x^2-5x-3=x^2+5x\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 2x^2-5x-3-x^2-5x=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow \;\; x^2-10x-3=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(2\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\;(v)\)

\(\quad (2x-1)(x-3)=(x+5)(x-1)\)

\(\;\Rightarrow 2x^2-6x-x+3=x^2-x+5x-5\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow 2x^2-7x+3=x^2+4x-5\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow 2x^2-7x+3-x^2-4x+5=0\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow x^2-11x+8=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(2\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\;\;(vi)\)

\(\qquad x^2+3x+1=(x-2)^2\)

\(\;\;\Rightarrow x^2+3x+1=x^2-2(x)(2)+2^2\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow x^2+3x+1=x^2-4x+4\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow x^2+3x+1-x^2+4x-4=0\)
\(\qquad\implies 7x-3=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(1\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;not\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\quad(vii)\)

\(\qquad (x+2)^3=2x(x^2-1)\)

\(\;\Rightarrow x^3+3(x)^2(2)+3(x)(2)^2+2^3=2x^3-2x\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow x^3+6x^2+12x+8=2x^3-2x\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow x^3+6x^2+12x+8-2x^3+2x=0\)
\(\;\;\Rightarrow -x^3+6x^2+14x+8=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(3\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;not\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\quad(viii)\)

\(\qquad x^3-4x^2-x+1=(x-2)^3\)

\(\Rightarrow x^3-4x^2-x+1=x^3-3(x)^2(2)+3(x)(2)^2-2^3\)
\(\Rightarrow x^3-4x^2-x+1=x^3-6x^2+12x-8\)
\(\Rightarrow x^3-4x^2-x+1-x^3+6x^2-12x+8=0\)

\(\quad\implies 2x^2-13x+9=0\)

\(Since\;the\;highest\;power\;of\) \(x\) \(is\) \(2\),
\(\quad So,\;it\;is\;a\;quadratic\) \(equation.\)

2. Represent the following situations in the form of quadratic equations:
(i) The area of a rectangular plot is 528 m². The length of the plot (in metres) is one more than twice its breadth. We need to find the length and breadth of the plot.
(ii) The product of two consecutive positive integers is 306. We need to find the integers.
(iii) Ram’s mother is 26 years older than him. The product of their ages (in years) 3 years from now will be 360. We would like to find Ram’s present age.
(iv) A train travels a distance of 480 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 8 km/h less, then it would have taken 3 hours more to cover the same distance. We need to find the speed of the train.

Solution:

\(\;\;(i)\)

\(\quad\)Let the breadth of the rectangular plot be \(x\) m.

\(\quad\)Then, the length of the plot \(=(2x+1)\) m.


\(\quad\)\(According\;to\;the\;question,\)

\(\qquad\qquad x(2x+1)=528\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow 2x^2+x=528\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow 2x^2+x-528=0\)


\(\quad\)\(Hence,\;the\;required\;quadratic\) \(equation\) \(is\) \(\;2x^2+x-528=0.\)



\(\;\;(ii)\)

\(\quad\)Let the first positive integer be \(x\).

\(\quad\)Then, the next consecutive positive integer is \((x+1)\).


\(\quad\)\(According\;to\;the\;question,\)

\(\qquad\qquad x(x+1)=306\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x^2+x=306\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x^2+x-306=0\)


\(\quad\)\(Hence,\;the\;required\;quadratic\) \(equation\) \(is\) \(\;x^2+x-306=0.\)



\(\;\;(iii)\)

\(\quad\)Let Ram's present age be \(x\) years.

\(\;\)Then, his mother's present age \(=(x+26)\) years.


\(\quad\)After \(3\) years, Ram's age \(=(x+3)\) years,
\(\quad\)and his mother's age \(=(x+29)\) years.


\(\quad\)\(According\;to\;the\;question,\)

\(\qquad\quad\; (x+3)(x+29)=360\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x^2+29x+3x+87=360\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x^2+32x+87=360\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x^2+32x+87-360=0\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x^2+32x-273=0\)


\(\quad\)\(Hence,\;the\;required\;quadratic\) \(equation\) \(is\) \(\;x^2+32x-273=0.\)



\(\;\;(iv)\)

\(\quad\)Let the speed of the train be \(x\) km/h.

\(\;\)Then, the reduced speed \(=(x-8)\) km/h.


\(\quad\)\(According\;to\;the\;question,\)


\(\qquad\qquad\;\; \dfrac{480}{x-8}=\dfrac{480}{x}+3\)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \dfrac{480}{x-8}-\dfrac{480}{x}=3\)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \dfrac{480x-480(x-8)}{x(x-8)}=3\)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \dfrac{480x-480x+3840}{x(x-8)}=3\)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \dfrac{3840}{x(x-8)}=3\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow 3840=3x(x-8)\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \dfrac{3840}{3}=x(x-8)\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x(x-8)=1280\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x^2-8x-1280=0\)


\(\quad\)\(Hence,\;the\;required\;quadratic\) \(equation\) \(is\) \(\;x^2-8x-1280=0.\)

3. For what value of p the equations \((p - 2 )x^2 + 3x + 5 = 0\) can not be quadratic?

\((a)\) \(\;1\) \(\;(b)\) \(\;2\) \(\;(c)\) \(\; - 2 \) \(\;(d)\) \(\; 0\)

Solution:\((b)\) \(2\) is the correct.



Explanatioin:

\(\quad\)Given,

\(\qquad (p-2)x^2+3x+5=0\)

\(\quad\)For the equation to be quadratic, the coefficient of \(x^2\) must not be zero.

\(\qquad\therefore p-2\neq0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow p\neq2\)

\(\quad\)Hence, the equation is not quadratic when

\(\qquad p-2=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow p=2\)

\(\therefore\) The correct option is \((b)\;2\).

4. Which of the following are quadratic equations?
\((i)\) \((x + 1)^2\) = \(2( x - 4)\)
\((ii)\) \((x - 3)( x + 1)\) = \(( x + 2)( x - 3)\)
\((iii)\) \((x - 2)^2 + 1\) = \(2x - 4\)
\((iv)\) \(x(x + 3) + 7\) = \(( x + 2)( x - 2)\)

\( choose \;the \;corect\; option.\)
\((a)\) \((i)\) and \((iv)\) \(\;(b)\) \((i)\) and \((ii)\)
\((c)\) \((i)\) and \((iii)\) \(\;(d)\) \((ii)\) and \((iv)\)

Solution:\((c)\) \((i)\) and \((iii)\) is correct



Explanation:

\(\quad(i)\)

\(\qquad (x+1)^2=2(x-4)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+2x+1=2x-8\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+2x+1-2x+8=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+9=0\)

\(\quad\)The highest power of \(x\) is \(2\).
\( Hence, \;it \;is \;a \;quadratic \;equation\).



\(\;(ii)\)

\(\quad\;\; (x-3)(x+1)=(x+2)(x-3)\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow x^2-2x-3=x^2-x-6\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x^2-2x-3-x^2+x+6=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow -x+3=0\)

\(\quad\)The highest power of \(x\) is \(1\).
\(Hence, \;it \;is \;not \;a \;quadratic\) \(equation.\)



\(\quad(iii)\)

\(\qquad (x-2)^2+1=2x-4\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-4x+4+1=2x-4\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-4x+5-2x+4=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-6x+9=0\)

\(\quad\)The highest power of \(x\) is \(2\).
\(Hence, \;it \;is \;a \;quadratic \;equation.\)



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\qquad x(x+3)+7=(x+2)(x-2)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+3x+7=x^2-4\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+3x+7-x^2+4=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 3x+11=0\)

\(\quad\)The highest power of \(x\) is \(1\).
\(Hence, \;it \;is \;not\; a \;quadratic\) \(equation.\)


\(\therefore\) The correct option is \((c)\) \((i)\) and \((iii)\).

Exercise 4.2

1. Find the roots of the following quadratic equations by factorisation:
\(\;(i)\) \(\; x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0\)
\(\;(ii)\) \(\; 2x^2 + x - 6 = 0\)
\(\;(iii)\) \(\; \sqrt{2}x^2 + 7x + 5\sqrt{2} = 0\)
\(\;(iv)\) \(\; 2x^2 - x + \frac{1}{8} = 0\)
\(\;(v)\) \(\; 100x^2 - 20x + 1 = 0\)
\(\;(vi)\) \(\; 2x^2 - 7x + 6 = 0\)
\(\;(vii)\) \(\; x^2 - 10x - 96 = 0\)
\(\;(viii)\) \(\; \sqrt{3}x^2 + 10x + 7\sqrt{3} = 0\)
\(\;(ix)\) \(\; x^2 + 2\sqrt{2}x + 2 = 0\)
\(\;(x)\) \(\; 14x + 5 - 3x^2 = 0\)

Solution:

\(\quad(i)\)

\(\qquad x^2-3x-10=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-5x+2x-10=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x(x-5)+2(x-5)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow (x-5)(x+2)=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x-5=0 \quad\text{or}\quad x+2=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x=5 \quad \text{or}\) \(\quad x=-2\)

\(\qquad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(5\) \(and\) \(-2\).



\(\quad(ii)\)

\(\qquad\quad 2x^2+x-6=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 2x^2+4x-3x-6=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 2x(x+2)-3(x+2)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow (x+2)(2x-3)=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x+2=0\quad \text{or}\quad 2x-3=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x=-2\quad \text{or}\quad x=\dfrac32\)

\(\qquad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(-2\) and \(\dfrac32\).



\(\quad(iii)\)

\(\qquad\quad \sqrt2x^2+7x+5\sqrt2=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow \sqrt2x^2+5x+2x+5\sqrt2=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow x(\sqrt2x+5)+\sqrt2(\sqrt2x+5)=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow (\sqrt2x+5)(x+\sqrt2)=0\)

\(\;\;\;\;\Rightarrow \sqrt2x+5=0\;\;\text{or}\;\;x+\sqrt2=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{-5}{\sqrt2}\quad \text{or}\quad x=-\sqrt2\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(\dfrac{-5}{\sqrt2}\) \(and\) \(-\sqrt2\).



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\qquad\quad 2x^2-x+\dfrac18=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow 8\left(2x^2-x+\dfrac18\right)=8(0)\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 16x^2-8x+1=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 16x^2-4x-4x+1=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 4x(4x-1)-1(4x-1)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow (4x-1)(4x-1)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 4x-1=0 \quad \text{or}\quad4x-1=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 4x=1\quad \text{or}\quad 4x=1\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x=\dfrac14 \quad \text{or}\quad x=\dfrac14\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(\;\dfrac14\) \(and\) \(\;\dfrac14\).



\(\quad(v)\)

\(\qquad\quad 100x^2-20x+1=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow 100x^2-10x-10x+1=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 10x(10x-1)-1(10x-1)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow (10x-1)(10x-1)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 10x-1=0 \;\; \text{or}\;\; 10x-1=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 10x=1 \quad \text{or}\quad 10x=1\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{1}{10} \quad \text{or}\quad x=\dfrac{1}{10}\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(\;\dfrac{1}{10}\) \(\;and\) \(\;\dfrac{1}{10}\).



\(\quad(vi)\)

\(\qquad\quad 2x^2-7x+6=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 2x^2-3x-4x+6=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x(2x-3)-2(2x-3)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow (2x-3)(x-2)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 2x-3=0\quad \text{or}\quad x-2=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x=\dfrac32\quad \text{or}\quad x=2\)
\(\qquad\therefore\) \(The\) \(roots\) \(are\) \(\dfrac32\) \(and\) \(2\).



\(\quad(vii)\)

\(\qquad\quad x^2-10x-96=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow x^2-16x+6x-96=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x(x-16)+6(x-16)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow (x-16)(x+6)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x-16=0\quad \text{or}\quad x+6=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x=16\quad \text{or}\quad x=-6\)
\(\qquad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(16\) \(and\) \(-6\).



\(\quad(viii)\)

\(\qquad\quad \sqrt3x^2+10x+7\sqrt3=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow \sqrt3x^2+7x+3x+7\sqrt3=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow x(\sqrt3x+7)+\sqrt3(\sqrt3x+7)=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow (\sqrt3x+7)(x+\sqrt3)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow \sqrt3x+7=0\;\;\text{or}\;\;x+\sqrt3=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{-7}{\sqrt3}\quad \text{or}\quad x=-\sqrt3\)

\(\qquad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(\dfrac{-7}{\sqrt3}\) \(and\) \(-\sqrt3\).



\(\quad(ix)\)

\(\qquad\quad x^2+2\sqrt2x+2=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow x^2+\sqrt2x+\sqrt2x+2=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow x(x+\sqrt2)+\sqrt2(x+\sqrt2)=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow (x+\sqrt2)(x+\sqrt2)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x+\sqrt2=0 \;\;\text{or}\;\;x+\sqrt2=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x=-\sqrt2\;\;\text{or}\;\;x=-\sqrt2\)

\(\quad\;\;\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(-\sqrt2\) \(and\) \(-\sqrt2\).


\(\quad(x)\)

\(\qquad\quad 14x+5-3x^2=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow 3x^2-14x-5=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 3x^2-15x+x-5=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 3x(x-5)+1(x-5)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow (x-5)(3x+1)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x-5=0\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;3x+1=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow x=5\quad \text{or}\quad x=-\dfrac13\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;roots \;are\) \(\;\) \(5\) \(and\) \(\;\) \(-\dfrac13\).

2. Solve the problems given in example 1.

\((i)\) \(John\) and \(Jayanti\) together have \(45\) marbles. Both of them lost \(5\) marbles each, and the product of the number of marbles they now have is \(124\) . We would like to find out how many marbles they had to start with.
\((ii)\) A cottage industry produces a certain number of toys in a day . The cost of production of each toy \((in \;rupees)\) was found to be \(55\) minus the number of toys produced in a day. On a particular day, the total cost of production was \(Rs \;750\). We would like to find out the number of toys produced on that day.

Solution:

\(\;\;\;(i)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Let John have \(x\) marbles initially.

\(\quad\)Then Jayanti had \((45-x)\) marbles initially.

\(\quad\;\)After losing \(5\) marbles each,

\(\quad\;\)John has \((x-5)\) marbles and Jayanti has \((40-x)\) marbles.

\(\quad\)\(According \;to \;the \;question\),

\(\qquad\quad (x-5)(40-x)=124\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow 40x-x^2-200+5x\)\(=\)\(124\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow -x^2+45x-200=124\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow -x^2+45x-200-124=0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow -x^2+45x-324=0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow x^2-45x+324=0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow x^2-36x-9x+324\)\(=\)\(0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow x(x-36)-9(x-36)\)\(=\)\(0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow (x-36)(x-9)=0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow x=36\quad\text{or}\quad x=9\)

\(\quad\)If John had \(36\) marbles, then Jayanti had \(45-36=9\) marbles.

\(\quad\)If John had \(9\) marbles, then Jayanti had \(45-9=36\) marbles.


\(\;Therefore,\;initially \;they \;had\) \(36\) \(and\) \(9\) \(marbles\).


\(\;\;\;(ii)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Let the number of toys produced in a day be \(x\).

\(\quad\)Then the cost of producing each toy is \((55-x)\) rupees.

\(\quad\)\(According \;to\; the \;question\),

\(\qquad\quad x(55-x)=750\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow 55x-x^2=750\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow x^2-55x+750\)\(=\)\(0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow x^2-30x-25x+750\)\(=\)\(0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow x(x-30)-25(x-30)\)\(=\)\(0\)
\(\quad\quad\Rightarrow (x-30)(x-25)=0\)
\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow x=30\quad \text{or}\quad x=25 \)

\(\quad\)If \(x=30\), then the cost of each toy is \(55-30=25\) rupees.

\(\quad\)If \(x=25\), then the cost of each toy is \(55-25=30\) rupees.


\(\;Therefore,\;the \;cottage\) \(industry\) \(produced\) \(either\) \(25\) \(toys\) \(at\) ₹\(30\) \(each\) \(or\) \(30\) \(toys\) \(at\) ₹\(25\) \(each\).

3. Find two numbers whose sum is 27 and product is 182.

Solution:

\(\qquad\)Let one number be \(x\).

\(\;\)Then, the other number is \((27-x)\).


\(According\;to\;the\;question,\)

\(\qquad\;\;\; x(27-x)=182\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 27x-x^2=182\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-27x+182=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-13x-14x+182=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x(x-13)-14(x-13)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow (x-13)\;\;(x-14)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x-13=0\;\;\text{or}\;\;x-14=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x=13\quad \text{or}\quad x=14\)

\(\;If \;x=13,\; then\; the\; other\) \(number\) \(is\)

\(\qquad 27-x=27-13=14 \)

\(\;If \;x=14,\;then \;the \;other\) \(number\) \(is\)

\(\qquad 27-x=27-14=13 \)

\(\;Therefore, \;the \;required\) \(numbers\) \(are\) \(13\) \(and\) \(14\).

4. Find two consecutive positive integers, sum of whose squares is 365.

Solution:

\(\quad\;\)Let the first positive integer be \(x\).

\(\quad\)Then, the next consecutive positive integer is \((x+1)\).


\(According\;to\;the\;question,\)

\(\qquad\quad x^2+(x+1)^2=365\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+x^2+2x+1=365\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 2x^2+2x-364=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+x-182=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2+14x-13x-182=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x(x+14)-13(x+14)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow (x+14)(x-13)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x+14=0\;\;\text{or}\;\;x-13=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x=-14\quad \text{or}\quad x=13\)


\(\;\)\(Since \;the \;integers \;are \;positive\), \(x=13\).

\(\;Therefore, \;the \;required\) \(consecutive\) \(positive\) \(integers\) \(are\) \(x=13\) and \(x+1=14\).

5. The altitude of a right triangle is 7 cm less than its base. If the hypotenuse is 13 cm, find the other two sides.

Solution:

\(\qquad\)Let the base of the right triangle be \(x\) cm.

\(\quad\)Then, the altitude is \((x-7)\) cm.


\(Applying\;pythagoras\;theorem\,\)

\(\qquad\quad x^2+(x-7)^2=13^2\)

\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow x^2+x^2-2(x)(7)+7^2=169\)
\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow 2x^2-14x+49=169\)
\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow 2x^2-14x+49-169=0\)
\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow 2x^2-14x-120=0\)
\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow x^2-7x-60=0\)
\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow x^2-12x+5x-60=0\)
\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow x(x-12)+5(x-12)=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow (-(k+4))^2-4(1)(2k+5)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow (k+4)^2-8k-20=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow k^2+8k+16-8k-20=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow k^2-4=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow k^2=4\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow k=\sqrt4\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow k=\pm2\)

\(\;Therefore \;the \;required\) \(values\) \(of\) \(k\) \(are\) \(\pm2\).



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\qquad\)Given equation,

\(\qquad\quad 2x^2+8x-k^3=0\)

\(\quad\;\;\;\)Here, \(\;a=2,\;b=8,\;c=-k^3\)

\(\qquad\)For equal roots, \(\;D=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow b^2-4ac=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 8^2-4(2)(-k^3)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 64+8k^3=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow k^3=-8\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow k=\sqrt[3]{-8}\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow k=-2\)

\(\;Therefore \;the \;required\) \(value\) \(of\) \(k\) \(is\) \(-2\).


\(\quad(v)\)

\(\qquad\)Given equation,

\(\qquad\quad (k-2)x^2+6x+9=0\)

\(\quad\;\)Here, \(\;a=k-2,\;b=6,\;c=9\)


\(\qquad\)\(For \;equal \;roots\), \(\;D=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow b^2-4ac=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 6^2-4(k-2)(9)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 36-36(k-2)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 36[1-(k-2)]=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 1-(k-2)=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 1-k+2=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 3-k=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow k=3\)

\(\;Therefore \;the \;required \;value\) \(of\) \(k\) \(is\) \(3\).


\(\quad(vi)\)

\(\qquad\)Given equation,

\(\;\;(k-12)x^2+2(k-12)x+2=0\)

\(\;\)Here,

\(\quad a=k-12,\;b=2(k-12)\), \(c=2\)


\(\qquad\)\(For \;equal \;roots\), \(\;D=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow b^2-4ac=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow [2(k-12)]^2-4(k-12)(2)\)\(=\)\(0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 4(k-12)^2-8(k-12)=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 4(k-12)\big[(k-12)-2\big]=0\)
\(\quad\Rightarrow 4(k-12)(k-14)=0\)

\(\quad\Rightarrow k=12\;\text{or}\;k=14\)

\(\quad\)For \(k=12\), the equation is not quadratic.

\(\;Therefore \;the \;required \;value\) \(of\) \(k\) \(is\) \(14\).

3. Is it possible to design a rectangular mango grove whose length is twice its breadth, and the area is \(800 \,m²\)? If so, find its length and breadth.

Solution:

\(\quad\)Then, the length of the mango grove is \(2x\) m.

\(\quad\)\(According \;to \;the \;question\),

\(\qquad\quad x\times(2x)=800\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 2x^2=800\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2=400\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x=\sqrt{400}\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x=\pm20\)

\(\quad\)Since the breadth cannot be negative,

\(\qquad\Rightarrow x=20\)

\(\quad\)Therefore, the length \(=2\times20\;m\) .
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=40\;m\)

\(\quad\therefore \;Yes, \;it \;is \;possible \;to\) \(design\) \(the\) \(mango\) \(grove\).

4. Is the following situation possible? If so, determine their present ages. The sum of the ages of two friends is \(20\) years. Four years ago, the product of their ages in years was \(48\).

Solution:

\(\quad\)Let the present age of one friend be \(x\) years.

\(\quad\)Then, the present age of the other friend is \((20-x)\) years.

\(\;\;\)Four years ago, their ages were \((x-4)\) years and \((16-x)\) years.

\(\quad\)\(According \;to \;the \;question\),

\(\qquad\quad (x-4)(16-x)=48\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 16x-x^2-64+4x=48\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow -x^2+20x-112=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow x^2-20x+112=0\)

\(\qquad\)Here, \(\;a=1,\;b=-20,\;c=112\)


\(\qquad\)\(Discriminant\), \(\;D=b^2-4ac\)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=(-20)^2-4(1)(112)\)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=400-448=-48\)

\(\qquad \)\(Since\), \(\;D< 0\)

\(\;Therefore, \,The \;situation \;is \; not\) \(possible.\)

5. Is it possible to design a rectangular park of perimeter \(80\) m and area \(400 \,m²\)? If so, find its length and breadth.

Solution:

\(\quad\)Let the breadth of the rectangular park be \(b\) m.

\(\quad\)Then, let the length of the rectangular park be \(l\) m.

\(\quad\)\(According \;to \;the \;perimeter\),

\(\qquad\quad 2(l+b)=80\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow l+b=40\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow l=40-b\)

\(\quad\)\(According \;to\; the \;area\),

\(\qquad\quad l\times b=400\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow (40-b)b=400\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow 40b-b^2=400\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow b^2-40b+400=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow b^2-20b-20b+400=0\)
\(\qquad\Rightarrow b(b-20)-20(b-20)=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow (b-20)(b-20)=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow b-20=0 \;\;\text{or}\;\;b-20=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow b=20\;\;\text{or}\;\;b=20\)

\(\quad\)\(Therefore\),

\(\qquad\qquad l=40-b=20\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow l=40-20=20\)

\(\quad\therefore\) Yes, it is possible to design the rectangular park.

\(\;So, \;the \;required \;length \;is\) \(20\;m\) \(and\) \(the\) \(breadth\) \(is\) \(20\;m\).

\((b)\) two equal real roots

\(\qquad\)Given,

\(\qquad\qquad a=9,\;b=-6,\;c=-2\)

8. \( Assertion \) \((A)\): If one roots of the quadratic equation \((k - 1)x^2 - 10x + 3 = 0 \) is reciprocal to the other root then \(k = 4\)

  \( Reason \) \((R)\): One root of the quadratic equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), \((a \neq0)\) reciprocal to the other only when \(a = c\)

  \(Choose \; the \; correct \; alternative: \)
  \((a)\) Both assertion \((A)\) and reason \((R)\) are true and \((R)\) is the correct explanation of assertion \((A)\).
  \((b)\) Both assertion \((A)\) and reason \((R)\) are true but \((R)\) is not the correct explanation of assertion \((A)\).
  \((c)\) Assertion \((A)\) is true but reason \((R)\) is false.
  \((d)\) Assertion \((A)\) is false but reason \((R)\) is true.

Solution:\((a)\) \(is \;the \;correct\) \(answer.\)



Explanation:

\(\quad\)For the quadratic equation

\(\quad\qquad (k-1)x^2-10x+3=0\)

\(\quad\)If one root is the reciprocal of the other, then

\(\qquad\qquad \alpha\beta=1\)

\(\quad\)But, for a quadratic equation,

\(\qquad\qquad \alpha\beta=\dfrac{c}{a}\)

\(\quad\qquad\Rightarrow \dfrac{3}{k-1}=1\)

\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow k-1=3\)

\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow k=4\)

\(\quad\therefore\) Assertion (A) is true.


\(\quad\)Now, for the quadratic equation

\(\quad\qquad ax^2+bx+c=0,\;(a\ne0)\)

\(\quad\)If one root is the reciprocal of the other, then

\(\qquad\qquad \alpha\beta=1\)

\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow \dfrac{c}{a}=1\)

\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow a=c\)

\(\quad\therefore\) Reason (R) is also true.

\(\quad\)Since the assertion follows directly from the reason,

\(\quad\therefore\) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

\(\quad\therefore\) The correct option is \((a)\).

NCERT Class 10 Maths Chapter 4 – Quadratic Equations FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Quadratic Equations.

What is a quadratic equation?

An equation of the form \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), where \(a \neq 0\).

How to find roots by factorisation?

Split the middle term, factor into linear factors, set each equal to zero.

What is the quadratic formula?

\(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\).

What does the discriminant tell us?

\(D = b^2-4ac\). If \(D>0\), two distinct real roots; \(D=0\), two equal real roots; \(D<0\), no real roots.

How to complete the square?

Rewrite \(ax^2+bx+c=0\) as \((x + \frac{b}{2a})^2 = \frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}\), then take square roots.

Can a quadratic have no real roots?

Yes, if discriminant < 0. The graph does not touch the x‑axis.

What is the sum and product of roots?

Sum = \(-b/a\), Product = \(c/a\).

How to form a quadratic from given roots \(\alpha, \beta\)?

The equation is \(x^2 - (\alpha+\beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0\).

What if \(a=0\) in quadratic equation?

Then it becomes a linear equation, not quadratic.

Why is completing the square useful?

It transforms the equation into a perfect square form, making it easier to solve and also leads to the quadratic formula.
