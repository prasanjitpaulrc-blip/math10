Surface Areas and Volumes is one of the most application‑oriented chapters in Class 10. You will learn to compute the surface areas and volumes of common 3D shapes like cuboids, cylinders, cones, spheres, and hemispheres, as well as their combinations and frustums. These concepts are widely used in engineering, architecture, and everyday life.

This chapter requires you to remember a set of formulas and, more importantly, understand how to break down complex objects into simpler parts. Mastering it will not only boost your exam scores but also enhance your spatial reasoning.

4. A pen stand made of wood is in the shape of a cuboid with four conical depressions to hold pens. The dimensions of the cuboid are \(15 \;cm\) by \(10\; cm\) by \(3.5\; cm\). The radius of each of the depressions is \(0.5\; cm\) and the depth is \(1.4\; cm\). Find the volume of wood in the entire stand \((see \;Fig.\; 12.16)\).

Solution: Volume of cuboid = 15 × 10 × 3.5 = 525 cm³. Volume of one conical depression = \(\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times (0.5)^2 \times 1.4 = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 0.25 \times 1.4 = \frac{1}{3} \times 22 \times 0.05 = \frac{1.1}{3} ≈ 0.3667 cm³\). Four depressions total = 4 × 0.3667 = 1.4668 cm³. Volume of wood = 525 − 1.4668 ≈ 523.53 cm³.

5. A vessel is in the form of an inverted cone. Its height is \(8\; cm\) and the radius of its top, which is open, is \(5 \;cm\). It is filled with water up to the brim. When lead shots, each of which is a sphere of radius \(0.5\; cm\) are dropped into the vessel, \(one-fourth\) of the water flows out. Find the number of lead shots dropped in the vessel.

Solution: Volume of cone = \(\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 25 \times 8 = \frac{4400}{21} ≈ 209.52 cm³\). Volume of water that flows out = \(\frac{1}{4} \times 209.52 = 52.38 cm³\). Volume of one lead shot = \(\frac{4}{3}\pi (0.5)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 0.125 = \frac{11}{21} ≈ 0.5238 cm³\). Number of shots = 52.38 / 0.5238 ≈ 100.

6. A solid iron pole consists of a cylinder of height \(220 \;cm\) and base diameter \(24\; cm,\) which is surmounted by another cylinder of height \(60 \;cm\) and radius \(8\; cm\). Find the mass of the pole, given that \(1\; cm^3\) of iron has approximately \(8\; g\) mass. \((Use \;\pi = 3.14)\)

Solution: Volume of larger cylinder = π(12)² × 220 = 3.14 × 144 × 220 = 99388.8 cm³. Volume of smaller cylinder = π(8)² × 60 = 3.14 × 64 × 60 = 12057.6 cm³. Total volume = 111446.4 cm³. Mass = 111446.4 × 8 g = 891571.2 g = 891.57 kg.

7. A solid consisting of a right circular cone of height \(120 \;cm\) and radius \(60 \;cm\) standing on a hemisphere of radius \(60\; cm\) is placed upright in a right circular cylinder full of water such that it touches the bottom. Find the volume of water left in the cylinder, if the cylinder has radius \(60\; cm\) and height \(180\; cm\).

Solution: Volume of cylinder = π(60)² × 180 = π × 3600 × 180 = 648000π cm³. Volume of solid = volume of cone + volume of hemisphere = \(\frac{1}{3}\pi (60)^2 (120) + \frac{2}{3}\pi (60)^3 = \pi \times 3600 \times (40 + 40) = \pi \times 3600 \times 80 = 288000\pi cm³\). Water left = 648000π − 288000π = 360000π cm³ = 360000 × 3.14 ≈ 1,130,400 cm³ = 1.13 m³.

8. A spherical glass vessel has a cylindrical neck \(8\; cm\) long, \(2 \;cm\) in diameter; the diameter of the spherical part is \(8.5 \;cm\). By measuring the amount of water it holds, a child finds its volume to be \(345\; cm^3\). Check whether she is correct, taking the above as the inside measurements, and \(\pi = 3.14\).

Solution: Volume of spherical part = \(\frac{4}{3}\pi (4.25)^3 = \frac{4}{3} \times 3.14 \times 76.765625 ≈ 321.39 cm³\). Volume of cylindrical neck = π(1)² × 8 = 3.14 × 8 = 25.12 cm³. Total volume = 321.39 + 25.12 = 346.51 cm³. Slightly more than 345 cm³, so child’s measurement is approximately correct (close).

NCERT Class 10 Math Chapter 13 – Surface Areas and Volumes FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Surface Areas and Volumes.

What is the formula for the surface area of a cuboid?

Total surface area = 2(lb + bh + hl); lateral surface area = 2h(l + b).

How do you find the volume of a cylinder?

Volume = πr²h.

What is the curved surface area of a cone?

Curved surface area = πrl, where l = √(r² + h²).

What is the volume of a sphere?

Volume = (4/3)πr³.

What is the volume of a frustum of a cone?

Volume = (1/3)πh (r₁² + r₂² + r₁r₂).

How to find the surface area of a hemisphere?

Curved surface area = 2πr²; total surface area = 3πr².

What is the slant height of a cone?

l = √(r² + h²).

How to calculate surface area of a combination of solids?

Add the areas of individual solids, then subtract overlapping areas.

What is the volume of a hemisphere?

Volume = (2/3)πr³.

What is the curved surface area of a cylinder?

Curved surface area = 2πrh.
