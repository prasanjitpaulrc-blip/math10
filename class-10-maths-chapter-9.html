Some Applications of Trigonometry is all about using trigonometric ratios to solve real‑world problems involving heights and distances. You will learn about the angle of elevation and the angle of depression, and how to set up right‑angled triangles to find unknown lengths. This chapter brings trigonometry out of the textbook and into the physical world — from measuring the height of a tower to finding the distance of a ship from the shore.

With only one exercise 9, this chapter is highly scoring. The key is to draw a clear diagram and correctly apply the tan, sin, or cos ratio. Once you grasp that, every problem becomes a simple equation.

Exercise 9

1. A circus artist is climbing a \(20\; m\) long rope, which is tightly stretched and tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. Find the height of the pole, if the angle made by the rope with the ground level is \(30^\circ\) \((see\; Fig.\; 9.11)\).

Solution:

\(\quad Let \;the\; height \;of\; the \;pole\; be\) \(AB=x\; m\).

\(\qquad\;\;Length \;of\; the \;rope,\) \(AC=20\; m\).

\(\qquad\;Angle \;made \;by \;the\; rope\; with\; the\) \(ground,\) \(\angle C=30^\circ\)


\(\quad\; Using \;the \;trigonometric \;ratio,\)

\(\qquad \sin30^\circ = \dfrac{Perpendicular}{Hypotenuse}=\dfrac{AB}{AC} \)


\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow \dfrac12=\dfrac{x}{20} \)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{20}{2} \)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=10\; m \)

\(\;\;\therefore\;The \;height \;of \;the\; pole \;is\;10\; m.\)

2. A tree breaks due to storm and the broken part bends so that the top of the tree touches the ground making an angle \(30^\circ\) with it. The distance between the foot of the tree to the point where the top touches the ground is \(8\; m\). Find the height of the tree.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;distance\;between\;the\;foot\;of\) \(the\) \(tree\) \(and\) \(the\) \(point\) \(where\) \(the\) \(top\) \(touches\) \(the\) \(ground,\) \(BC=8\;m.\)

\(\quad\;Angle\;made\;by\;the\;broken\;part\;with\) \(the\) \(ground,\) \(\angle\) \(C=30^\circ.\)

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;remaining\;part\) \(of\) \(the\) \(tree\) \(be\) \(AB=x\;m\) \(and\) \(the\) \(broken\) \(part\) \(be\) \(AC=l\;m.\)


\(\qquad Using\;the\;trigonometric\;ratio,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\;\; \tan30^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BC} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3} = \dfrac{x}{8} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; x=\dfrac{8}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{8\times\sqrt3}{\sqrt3 \times\sqrt3} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; x\;=\;\dfrac{8\sqrt3}{3}\;m \)


\(\quad Again, \;using \;the \;trigonometric \;ratio, \)

\(\qquad\qquad \cos30^\circ = \dfrac{BC}{AC} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = \dfrac{8}{l} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; l=\dfrac{16}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{16\times \sqrt3}{\sqrt3\times \sqrt3} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; l=\dfrac{16\sqrt3}{3}\;m \)


\(\;\; Now,\)
\(\qquad Height \;of \;the \;tree = x+l \)

\(\qquad\qquad = \dfrac{8\sqrt3}{3}+\dfrac{16\sqrt3}{3} \)


\(\qquad\qquad = \dfrac{24}{\sqrt3} = 8\sqrt3\;m \)


\(\;\;\therefore\; The \;height \;of \;the \;tree \;is \;8\sqrt3\;m. \)

3. A contractor plans to install two slides for the children to play in a park. For the children below the age of \(5\) years, she prefers to have a slide whose top is at a height of \(1.5 \;m,\) and is inclined at an angle of \(30^\circ\) to the ground, whereas for elder children, she wants to have a steep slide at a height of \(3 \;m,\) and inclined at an angle of \(60^\circ\) to the ground. What should be the length of the slide in each case?

Solution:

\(\quad (i)\;For\;the\;slide\;for\;children\;below\) \(5\) \(years,\)

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;slide\;be\;AB=1.5\;m\)

\(\quad Angle\;made\;by\;the\;slide\;with\;the\) \(ground,\) \(\angle C=30^\circ\)

\(\quad Let\;the\;length\;of\;the\;slide\;be\) \(AC\)\(=\)\(l\;m.\)

\(\qquad Using\;the\;trigonometric\;ratio,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \sin30^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{AC} \)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; \dfrac12 = \dfrac{1.5}{l} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; l=1.5\times2 \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; l=3\;m \)

\(\quad\therefore\; The \;length \;of \;the \;slide \;for\) \(children\) \(below\) \(5\) \(years\) \(is\) \(3\;m. \)



\(\quad (ii)\;For\;the\;slide\;for\;elder\;children,\)

\(\qquad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;slide\;be\;AB=3\;m\)

\(\quad Angle\;made\;by\;the\;slide\;with\;the\) \(ground,\) \(\angle C=60^\circ\)

\(\quad Let\;the\;length\;of\;the\;slide\;be\) \(AC\)\(=\)\(l\;m.\)

\(\qquad Using\;the\;trigonometric\;ratio,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \sin60^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{AC} \)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = \dfrac{3}{l} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; l=\dfrac{6}{\sqrt3} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; l=\dfrac{6\times \sqrt3}{\sqrt3\times \sqrt3} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; l=2\sqrt3\;m \)

\(\quad\therefore\; The \;length \;of\; the\; slide \;for\) \(elder\) \(children\) \(is\) \(2\sqrt3\;m. \)

4. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground, which is \(30 \;m\) away from the foot of the tower, is \(30^\circ\). Find the height of the tower.

Solution:

\(\;\; Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;tower\;be\;AB=\) \(x.\)

\(\quad Distance\;between\;the\;point\;and\;the\) \(foot\) \(of\) \(the\) \(tower,\) \(BC=30\; m\).

\(\quad Angle\;of\;elevation\;of\;the\) \(top\) \(of\) \(the\) \(tower,\) \(\angle C=30^\circ.\)


\(\quad Using\;the\;trigonometric\;ratio,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{BC}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{x}{30}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{30}{\sqrt3}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{30\times \sqrt3}{\sqrt3\times \sqrt3}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{30\sqrt3}{3}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=10\sqrt3\;m\)

\(\;\therefore\;The\;height\;of\;the\;tower\) \(is\) \(10\sqrt3\;m.\)

5. A kite is flying at a height of \(60 \;m\) above the ground. The string attached to the kite is temporarily tied to a point on the ground. The inclination of the string with the ground is \(60^\circ\). Find the length of the string, assuming that there is no slack in the string.

Solution:

\(\;\; Let\;the\;length\;of\;the\;string\;be\;AC=x.\)

\(\quad Height\;of\;the\;kite,\;AB=60\;m.\)

\(\;\; Angle\;of\;inclination\;of\;the\;string\) \(with\) \(the\) \(ground,\) \(\angle C=60^\circ.\)


\(\quad Using\;the\;trigonometric\;ratio,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \sin60^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{AC}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\dfrac{60}{x}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{60\times2}{\sqrt3}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{120}{\sqrt3}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{120\times \sqrt3}{\sqrt3\times \sqrt3}\)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{120\sqrt3}{3}\)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=40\sqrt3\;m\)


\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;length\;of\;the\;string\) \(is\) \(40\sqrt3\;m.\)

6. A \(1.5 \;m\) tall boy is standing at some distance from a \(30\; m\) tall building. The angle of elevation from his eyes to the top of the building increases from \(30^\circ\) to \(60^\circ\) as he walks towards the building. Find the distance he walked.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;initial\;distance\;of\;the\;boy\) \(from\) \(the\) \(building\) \(be\) \(BC\) \(=\) \(x\;m.\)

\(\quad\; Height\;of\;the\;building\;AB=30\;m.\)

\(\quad\; Height\;of\;the\;boy\;CD=1.5\;m.\)

\(\;\therefore\; Height\;from\;the\;boy's\;eyes\;to\) \(the\) \(top\) \(of\) \(the\) \(building\) \(AF=AB-FB\) \(=30-1.5=28.5\;m.\)


\(\quad\;For\;the\;initial\;position,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{AF}{DF}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{28.5}{x}\)

\(\;\;\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[\because DF\; and\;CB\; are \;the \;opposite\)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\; \;sides \;of \;the \;rectangle \;BCDF,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;\; \;so \;DF=CB=x]\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=28.5\sqrt3\;m.\)


\(\quad\;Let\;the\;final\;distance\;of\;the\;boy\) \(from\) \(the\) \(building\) \(be\) \(EF\) \(=\) \(y\;m.\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan60^\circ=\dfrac{AF}{EF}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{28.5}{y}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow y=\dfrac{28.5}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{28.5\times\sqrt3}{\sqrt3\times\sqrt3}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow y=\dfrac{28.5\sqrt3}{3}=9.5\sqrt3\;m.\)


\(\quad\;Distance\;walked=x-y\)

\(\qquad\qquad =28.5\sqrt3-9.5\sqrt3\)

\(\qquad\qquad =19\sqrt3\;m.\)

\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;distance\;walked\;by\) \(the\) \(boy\) \(is\) \(19\sqrt3\;m.\)

7. From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a transmission tower fixed at the top of a \(20\; m\) high building are \(45^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\) respectively. Find the height of the tower.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;transmission\) \(tower\) \(be\) \(AD\) \(=\) \(h\;m.\)

\(\quad Height\;of\;the\;building\;AB=20\;m.\)

\(\quad\;Let\;the\;horizontal\;distance\;from\) \(the\) \(point\) \(to\) \(the\) \(building\) \(be\) \(BC\) \(=\) \(x\;m.\)


\(\quad\;In\;right-angled\;\triangle ABC,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan45^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{BC}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 1=\dfrac{20}{x}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=20\;m \qquad\ldots{(i)}\)


\(\;\;Again,\;in \;right-angled\;\triangle DBC,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan60^\circ=\dfrac{DB}{BC}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{h+20}{x}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{h+20}{20}\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[from\; eq^n(i)]\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 20\sqrt3=h+20\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 20\sqrt3-20 = h\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 20(\sqrt3-1)\;m=h.\)


\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;height\;of\;the\;transmission\) \(tower\) \(is\) \(20(\sqrt3-1)\;m.\)

8. A statue, \(1.6 \;m\) tall, stands on the top of a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the statue is \(60^\circ\) and from the same point the angle of elevation of the top of the pedestal is \(45^\circ\). Find the height of the pedestal.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;AB=h\;m \;be\;the\;height\;of\;the\;pedestal\) \(and\) \(BC\)\(=\)\(x\;m\) \(be\) \(the\) \(distance\) \(of\) \(the\) \(point\) \(from\) \(the\) \(pedestal.\)

\(\quad\; Height\;of\;the\;statue\;be\;AD\)\(=\)\(1.6\;m.\)


\(\quad\;In \;right-angled\;\triangle ABC,\)

\(\qquad\quad \tan45^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BC} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow 1=\dfrac{h}{x} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x\;=\;h\qquad\ldots{(i)} \)


\(\;\;Again,\;in \;right-angled\;\triangle CBD,\)

\(\qquad\quad \tan60^\circ= \dfrac{DB}{BC} = \dfrac{h+1.6}{x} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{h+1.6}{h} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[from\; eq^n(i)]\)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \sqrt3h=h+1.6 \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \sqrt3h-h=1.6 \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h(\sqrt3-1)=1.6 \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\dfrac{1.6}{\sqrt3-1} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\dfrac{1.6(\sqrt3+1)}{(\sqrt3-1)(\sqrt3+1)} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\dfrac{1.6(\sqrt3+1)}{(\sqrt3)^2-1^2} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\dfrac{1.6(\sqrt3+1)}{3-1} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\dfrac{1.6(\sqrt3+1)}{2} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h\;=\;0.8(\sqrt3+1)\;m \)


\(\therefore\;The \;height\; of\; the \;pedestal\; is\) \(0.8(\sqrt3+1)\;m.\)

9. The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of the tower is \(30^\circ\) and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is \(60^\circ\). If the tower is \(50\; m\) high, find the height of the building.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;building\;be\) \(AB\)\(=\)\(h\;m.\)

\(\quad\; Height\;of\;the\;tower,\;CD=50\;m.\)

\(\quad\; Distance\;between\;the\;tower\;and\;the\) \(building\) \(be\) \(BC\)\(=\)\(x\;m.\)


\(\quad\;In \;right-angled\;\triangle ABC,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{BC}\)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{h}{x} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=h\sqrt3 \qquad\ldots\;{(i)} \)


\(\;\;Again,\;in \;right-angled\;\triangle ACD,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan60^\circ\;=\;\dfrac{CD}{BC}\)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \sqrt3\;=\;\dfrac{50}{x} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{50}{\sqrt3} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h\sqrt3=\dfrac{50}{\sqrt3} \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[from\; eq^n(i)]\)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\dfrac{50}{\sqrt3\times\sqrt3} \)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\dfrac{50}{3} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=16\dfrac{2}{3}\;m \)

\(\therefore\;\text{The height of the building is }16\dfrac{2}{3}\;m.\)

10. Two poles of equal heights are standing opposite each other on either side of the road, which is \(80 \;m\) wide. From a point between them on the road, the angles of elevation of the top of the poles are \(60^\circ\) and \(30^\circ\), respectively. Find the height of the poles and the distances of the point from the poles.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;each\;pole\;be\) \(DC=AB=\)\(h\;m.\)

\(\quad\;Then,\;the\;distance\;of\;the\;point\;from\) \(the\) \(second\) \(pole\) \(is\) \(EB\)\(=\)\((80-x)\)\(m.\)

\(\;\;Let\;the\;angles\;of\;elevation\;from\) \(the\) \(point\) \('E'\) \(to\) \(the\) \(pole\) \(AB\) \(be\) \(\angle AED=60^\circ\) \(and\) \(from\) \(the\) \(pole\) \(DC\) \(be\) \(\angle CED=30^\circ.\)


\(\quad\;In \;right-angled\;\triangle ABE,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan60^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BE} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3\;=\;\dfrac{h}{x} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \;\;h\;\;=\;\;\sqrt3x\qquad\ldots\;{(i)} \)


\(\;\;Again,\;in \;right-angled\;\triangle DCE,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan30^\circ\; =\; \dfrac{DC}{CE} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3} = \dfrac{h}{80-x} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \;\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3} = \dfrac{h}{80-x} \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 80-x=\sqrt3h \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 80-x=\sqrt3(\sqrt3x) \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[from\; eq^n(i)]\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 80-x=3x \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 4x=80 \)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=20\;m \)


\(\quad\;Therefore,\;the\;distance\;of\;the\;point\) \(from\) \(the\) \(first\) \(pole\) \(BE\) \(is\) \(20\;m.\)

\(\quad\;Distance\;from\;the\;second\;pole\) \(CE\) \(=80-20=60\;m.\)


\(\quad\;Now,\;h=\sqrt3x\)

\(\qquad\;\;\; \Rightarrow h=\sqrt3\times20\;m=20\sqrt3\;m \)


\(\therefore\;The \;height\; of \;each \;pole \;is \;20\sqrt3\;m.\)

\(\;And\;the \;distances \;of \;the \;point \;from\) \(the\) \(two\) \(poles\) \(are\) \(20\;m\) \(and\) \(60\;m,\) \(respectively.\)

11. A TV tower stands vertically on a bank of a canal. From a point on the other bank directly opposite the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is \(60^\circ\). From another point \(20 \;m\) away from this point on the line joining this point to the foot of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is \(30^\circ\) \((see \;Fig. \;9.12)\). Find the height of the tower and the width of the canal.

Solution:

\(\;\; Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;tower\;be\) \(AB\)\(=\)\(h\;m.\)

\(\;\; And\;the\;width\;of\;the\;canal\) \(be\) \(BC\)\(=\)\(x\;m.\)

\(\qquad Given\; CD=20\;m.\)


\(\quad\;From\;the\;point\;C,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan60^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{BC}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{h}{x}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow\; h\;=\;\)\(x\sqrt{3} \quad\ldots{(i)}\)


\(\quad\;From\;the\;point\;D,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan30^\circ\;=\;\dfrac{AB}{BD}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{h}{x+20}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{x\sqrt3}{x+20}\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[from\; eq^n(i)]\)

\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow {x+20}=x\sqrt3\times\sqrt3\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x+20=3x\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 20=3x-x\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 20=2x\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{20}{2}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=10\;m\)

\(\; Now,\)

\(\quad\;Substituting\;x=10\;m\;in\;(i),\)

\(\qquad\qquad h=10\sqrt3\;m\)

\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;width\;of\;the\;canal\;is\) \(10\;m\) \(and\) \(the\) \(height\) \(of\) \(the\) \(tower\) \(is\) \(10\sqrt3\;m.\)

12. From the top of a \(7 \;m\) high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is \(60^\circ\) and the angle of depression of its foot is \(45^\circ\). Determine the height of the tower.

Solution:

\(\;\; Let\;AB=7\;m\;be\;the\;height\;of\;the\) \(building\) \(and\) \(CD=h\;m\) \(be\) \(the\) \(height\) \(of\) \(the\) \(tower\)

\(\quad\therefore ED=7\;m \;and\;CE=(h-7)\;m\)

\(\;\; Let\;the\;horizontal\;distance\;between\) \(the\) \(building\) \(and\) \(the\) \(tower\) \(be\) \(BD\)\(=AE\)\(=\)\(x\;m.\)


\(\quad\;In\;right-angled\;\triangle\;AED,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan45^\circ=\dfrac{ED}{AE}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 1=\dfrac{7}{x}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \;x\;=\;7\;m \qquad \ldots{(i)}\)


\(\;\;Again,\;in\;right-angled\;\triangle\;AEC,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan60^\circ\;=\;\dfrac{AE}{CE}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{h-7}{x}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{h-7}{7}\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad[from\; eq^n(i)]\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 7\sqrt3 = h-7\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 7\sqrt3+7 = h\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 7(\sqrt3+1) = h\)


\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;height\;of\;the\;tower\;is\) \(7(1+\sqrt3)\;m.\)

13. As observed from the top of a \(75 \;m\) high lighthouse from the sea‑level, the angles of depression of two ships are \(30^\circ\) and \(45^\circ\). If one ship is exactly behind the other on the same side of the lighthouse, find the distance between the two ships.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\) \(lighthouse\) \(be\) \(AB=75\;m.\)

\(\quad\;Let\;the\;distances\;of\;the\;first\) \(ships\) \(from\) \(the\) \(foot\) \(of\) \(the\) \(lighthouse\) \(be\) \(BC=x\) \(m\) \(and\) \(the\) \(distance\) \(between\) \(the\) \(two\) \(ships\) \(be\) \(CD=y\;m.\)


\(\quad\;In\;right\;triangle\;ABC,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\; \tan45^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{BC}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 1=\dfrac{75}{x}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=75\;m\)


\(\quad\;In\;right\;triangle\;ABD,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{BD}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{75}{y+x}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{75}{y+75}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow y+75=75\sqrt3\;m\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow y = 75\sqrt3-75\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow y=75(\sqrt3-1)\;m\)


\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;distance\;between\;the\) \(two\) \(ships\) \(is\) \(75(\sqrt3-1)\;m.\)

14. A \(1.2 \;m\) tall girl spots a balloon moving with the wind in a horizontal line at a height of \(88.2\; m\) from the ground. The angle of elevation of the balloon from the eyes of the girl at any instant is \(60^\circ\). After some time, the angle of elevation reduces to \(30^\circ\) \((see \;Fig. \;9.13)\). Find the distance travelled by the balloon during the interval.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;distance\;of\;the\;balloon\) \(from\) \(the\) \(girl\) \(at\) \(the\) \(first\) \(position\) \(be\) \(AB=x\;m,\) \(and\) \(the\) \(distance\) \(of\) \(the\) \(balloon\) \(from\) \(the\) \(girl\) \(at\) \(the\) \(second\) \(position\) \(be\) \(AE=y\;m.\)

\(\quad\; Height\;of\;the\;balloon\;from\) \(the\) \(ground\) \(=\) \(88.2\;m.\)

\(\quad\; Height\;of\;the\;girl=1.2\;m.\)

\(\; \therefore\;Height\;of\;the\;balloon\;from\;the\) \(eyes\) \(of\) \(the\) \(girl\)\(=\)\(88.2-1.2=87\;m.\)


\(\quad In\;right-angled\triangle\;ABC,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan60^\circ\;=\;\dfrac{BC}{AB}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \sqrt3=\dfrac{87}{x}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{87}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{87\sqrt3}{\sqrt3\times\sqrt3}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{87\sqrt3}{3}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=29\sqrt3\;m\)


\(\;\;Again,\;in\;right-angled\;\triangle\;AED,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{DE}{AE}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{87}{y}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow y=87\sqrt3\;m\)


\(\quad Distance\;travelled\;by\;the\;balloon,\) \(BE=y-x\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow BE=87\sqrt3-29\sqrt3\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow BE=58\sqrt3\;m\)

\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;distance\;travelled\;by\;the\) \(balloon\) \(is\) \(58\sqrt3\;m.\)

15. A straight highway leads to the foot of a tower. A man standing at the top of the tower observes a car at an angle of depression of \(30^\circ,\) which is approaching the foot of the tower with a uniform speed. Six seconds later, the angle of depression of the car is found to be \(60^\circ\). Find the time taken by the car to reach the foot of the tower from this point.

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;tower\) \(DC\) \(be\) \(h\;m\) \(and\) \(the\) \(speed\) \(of\) \(the\) \(car\) \(be\) \(x\;m/s.\)

\(\quad Then,\;the\;distance\;covered\) \(by\) \(the\) \(car\) \(in\) \(6\;s\) \(will\) \(be\) \(6x\;m.\)

\(\quad Also,\;let\;the\;time\;taken\) \(by\) \(the\) \(car\) \(to\) \(move\) \(from\) \(B\) \(to\) \(C\) \(be\) \(t\;s.\)

\(\quad \therefore\;BC=xt\;m.\)


\(\quad In \;right-angled\triangle\; DCB,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan 60^\circ=\dfrac{DC}{BC} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \sqrt{3}=\dfrac{h}{xt} \)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=\sqrt{3}xt \qquad\ldots(i) \)


\(\;\;Again,\;in\;right-angled\triangle \;DCA,\)

\(\qquad\quad \tan 30^\circ=\dfrac{DC}{AC} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \;=\;\dfrac{h}{6x+xt} \)


\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow 6x+xt=\sqrt{3}h \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow x(6+t)=\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}xt \)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad [from\;(i)] \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow 6+t=3t \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow 2t=6 \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow t=3\;s \)


\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;time\;taken\;by\;the\;car\) \(to\) \(reach\) \(the\) \(foot\) \(of\) \(the\) \(tower\) \(is\) \(3\;seconds.\)

16. The angles of elevation of the top of a tower from two points at a distance of \(4\; m\) and \(9\; m\) from the base of the tower and in the same straight line with it are complementary. Prove that the height of the tower is \(6\; m\).

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;tower\) \(be\) \(AB\)\(=\)\(h\;m,\) \(and\) \(PB\)\(=\)\(4\;m\) \(and\) \(QB\)\(=\)\(9\;m\) \(are\) \(the\) \(distances\) \(of\) \(the\) \(points\) \(P\) \(and\) \(Q\) \(from\) \(the\) \(base\) \(of\) \(the\) \(tower.\)

\(\quad\; Let\;the\;angles\;of\;elevation\) \(from\) \(P\) \(and\) \(Q\) \(be\) \(\theta\) \(and\) \((90^\circ-\theta)\) \(respectively.\)


\(\quad\;In\;the\;right-angled\triangle\;PBA,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan\theta=\dfrac{AB}{PB}\)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \tan\theta=\dfrac{h}{4} \quad \ldots (i)\)


\(\;\;Again,\;in\;right-angled\triangle\;QBA,\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan(90^\circ-\theta)=\dfrac{AB}{QB}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \cot\theta=\dfrac{h}{9}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta}\;=\;\dfrac{h}{9}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\dfrac{h}{4}}=\dfrac{h}{9}\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad (from\;equation\;(i))\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{4}{h}=\dfrac{h}{9}\)


\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow h^2=36\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow h^2=6^2\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow h=6\;m\)

\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;height\;of\;the\;tower\;is\;6\;m.\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad Hence,\;proved.\)

17. A lighthouse stands tall on a cliff by the sea, watching over ships that pass by. One day a ship is seen approaching the shore and from the top of the lighthouse, the angles of depression of the ship are observed to be \(30^\circ\) and \(45^\circ\) as it moves from point \(P\) to point \(Q\). The height of the lighthouse is \(50\;metres.(Fig.9.14)\)

Based on the information given above, answer the following questions:
\((i)\) Find the distance of the ship from the base of the lighthouse when it is at point \(Q\). where the angle of depression is \(45^\circ\)
\((ii)\) Find the measures of \(\angle PBA\) and \(\angle QBA\)
\((iii)\) Find the distance travelled by the ship between points \(P\) and \(Q\).
\((iv)\) If the ship continues moving towards the shore and takes \(10\) minutes to travel from \(Q\) to \(A\), calculate the speed of the ship in km/h, from \(Q\) to \(A\)

Solution:

\(\quad Let\;the\;height\;of\;the\;lighthouse\) \(be\) \(AB\)\(=\)\(50\;m\) \(and\) \(the\) \(distances\) \(of\) \(the\) \(ship\) \(from\) \(the\) \(base\) \(of\) \(the\) \(lighthouse\) \(at\) \(P\) \(and\) \(Q\) \(be\) \(AP\)\(=\)\(x\;m\) \(and\) \(AQ\)\(=\)\(y\;m\) \(respectively.\)


\(\quad (i)\;When\;the\;ship\;is\;at\;Q,\;the\) \(angle\) \(of\) \(depression\) \(is\) \(45^\circ.\)

\(\qquad\qquad \therefore\;\angle BQA=45^\circ\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan45^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{AQ}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow 1=\dfrac{50}{y}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow y=50\;m\)

\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;distance\;of\;the\;ship\) \(from\) \(the\) \(base\) \(of\) \(the\) \(lighthouse\) \(at\) \(Q\) \(is\) \(50\) \(m.\)


\(\quad\;(ii)\;Since\;the\;horizontal\;line\;through\) \(B\) \(is\) \(parallel\) \(to\) \(PA\) \(and\) \(QA,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \angle PBA=90^\circ-30^\circ=60^\circ\)

\(\qquad\qquad \angle QBA=90^\circ-45^\circ=45^\circ\)

\(\quad\therefore\;\angle PBA=60^\circ\;and\;\angle QBA=45^\circ.\)


\(\quad\;(iii)\;When\;the\;ship\;is\;at\;P,\) \(the\) \(angle\) \(of\) \(depression\) \(is\) \(30^\circ.\)

\(\qquad\qquad\quad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{AB}{AP}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}=\dfrac{50}{x}\)

\(\qquad\qquad \Rightarrow x=50\sqrt3\;m\)


\(\quad\;Distance\;travelled\;by\;the\) \(ship\) \(between\) \(P\) \(and\) \(Q\) \(is\)

\(\qquad\qquad AP-AQ=50\sqrt3-50\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad = 50(\sqrt3-1)\;m\)

\(\;\;\therefore\;The\;distance\;travelled\;by\;the\) \(ship\) \(between\) \(P\) \(and\) \(Q\) \(is\) \(50(\sqrt3-1)\;m.\)


\(\;\;(iv)\;Distance\;from\;Q\;to\;A=50\;m\)

\(\qquad\qquad \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad =0.05\;km.\)

\(\quad Time=10\;minutes=\dfrac{10}{60}\;hour\)

\(\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad =\dfrac{1}{6}\;hour.\)


\(\qquad\;\;\therefore\; Speed\;=\;\dfrac{Distance}{Time}\)

\(\qquad \Rightarrow Speed=\dfrac{0.05}{\dfrac{1}{6}}\;km/h\)

\(\qquad \Rightarrow Speed=0.05\times6\;km/h\)

\(\qquad \Rightarrow Speed=0.3\;km/h\)

\(\;\therefore\;The\;speed\;of\;the\;ship\;from\) \(Q\) \(to\) \(A\) \(is\) \(0.3\;km/h.\)

18. A \(30\;m\) long rope is tightly stretched and tied from the top pole to the ground. If the rope makes an angle of \(60^\circ\) with the ground, the height of the pole is:
\(\quad(a)\) \(10\sqrt{3}\;m\) \(\qquad(b)\) \(30\sqrt{3}\;m\)
\(\quad(c)\) \(15\;m\) \(\qquad\quad(d)\) \(15\sqrt{3}\;m\)

Solution:\(Option\;(d)15\sqrt{3}\; is\; the\; correct.\)


Explanation:

\(\qquad\qquad \sin 60^\circ=\dfrac{Height \;of \;pole}{Length \;of \;rope} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}=\dfrac{h}{30} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=30\times\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \)

\(\qquad\quad \Rightarrow h=15\sqrt{3}\;m \)

\(\;\; \therefore The \;correct \;answer \;is\) \((d)\) \(15\sqrt{3}\;m\)

19. If a pole \(6\;m\) high casts a shadow \(2\sqrt{3}\;m\) long on the ground then sun's elevation is
\(\quad(a)\) \(60^\circ\) \(\qquad\quad(b)\) \(45^\circ\)
\(\quad(c)\) \(30^\circ\) \(\qquad\quad(d)\) \(90^\circ\)

Solution:\(Option\;(a) 60^\circ\; is\; the\; correct.\)


Explanation:

\(\qquad\qquad \tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{Height of pole}}{\text{Length of shadow}} \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \tan\theta=\dfrac{6}{2\sqrt{3}} \)


\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \tan\theta=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{3}}=\dfrac{3\times\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}} \)


\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \tan\theta=\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{3} \)


\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \tan\theta=\sqrt{3} \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \tan\theta=\tan60^\circ \)

\(\qquad\qquad \therefore\theta=60^\circ \)

20. The angle of elevation of a tower from two points at a distance of \(a\) and \(b\) \((a > b)\) from its foot and in the same straight line from it are \(30^\circ\) and \(60^\circ,\) then the height of the tower is:
\(\quad(a)\) \(\sqrt{a+b}\) \(\qquad\quad(b)\) \(\sqrt{a-b}\)
\(\quad(c)\) \(\sqrt{ab}\) \(\qquad\qquad(d)\) \(\sqrt{\dfrac{a}{b}}\)

Solution:\(Option\;(c) \sqrt{ab}\; is\; the\; correct.\)


Explanation:

\(\qquad From\;the\;point\;a,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{\text{Height of tower}}{\text{Distance from foot}} \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}=\dfrac{h}{a} \)


\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow h=\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{3}}\qquad\ldots(i) \)


\(\qquad From\;the\;point\;b,\)

\(\qquad\qquad \tan60^\circ=\dfrac{h}{b} \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \sqrt{3}=\dfrac{h}{b} \)


\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow h=b\sqrt{3}\qquad\ldots(ii) \)


\(\quad Multplying \; (i) \;and\;(ii),\;we\;get\)

\(\qquad\qquad\;\; h^2=\dfrac{a}{\sqrt{3}}\times b\sqrt{3} \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow h^2=ab \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow h=\sqrt{ab} \)


\(\;\; \therefore The \;correct \;answer \;is\; (c) \sqrt{ab} \)

21. In the following question a statement of assertion \((A)\) is followed by a statement of reason \((R)\):

\(\;\; Assertion\;(A):\) A ladder leaning against a wall, stands at a horizontal distance of \(6\;m\) from the wall. If the height of the wall up to which the ladder reaches is \(8\;m,\) then the length of the ladder is \(10\;m\).
\(\;\;Reason\;(R):\) The ladder makes an angle of \(60^\circ\) with the ground.

\(Choose\;the\;correct\;option\;from\) \(the\) \(given\) \(alternatives\):
\(\;\; (a)\) Both Assertion \((A)\) and Reason \((R)\) are true and Reason \((R)\) is the correct explanation of the Assertion \((A)\).
\(\;\; (b)\) Both Assertion \((A)\) and Reason \((R)\) are true but Reason \((R)\) is not the correct explanation of Assertion \((A)\).
\(\;\; (c)\) Assertion \((A)\) is true, but Reason \((R)\) is false.
\(\;\; (d)\) Assertion \((A)\) is false but Reason \((R)\) is true.

Solution:\(Option\;(c)\;Assertion\;(A)\) \(is\) \(true\) \(but\) \(Reason\) \((R)\) \(is\) \(false\)


Explanation:

\(\quad Assertion(A):\)

\(\quad \text{Ladder's length}^2=(\text{Height})^2+(\text{Base})^2 \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow l^2=8^2+6^2 \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow l^2=64+36=100 \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow l^2==10^2 \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow l=10\;m \)


\(\quad \therefore\;Assertion\;(A)\;\text{is true.} \)


\(\quad Reason(R):\)

\(\qquad \tan\theta=\dfrac{8}{6}=\dfrac{4}{3}\ne\sqrt{3}=\tan60^\circ \)


\(\quad \therefore\;Reason\;(R)\;\text{is false.} \)

22. \(Statement\) \((i):\) If the angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground \(100\;m\) away from the foot of the tower is \(30^\circ\) then height of the tower is \(100\sqrt{3}\;m\).
\(Statement\;(ii):\) If the length of the shadow of a vertical pole is equal to its height then the angle of elevation of Sun's altitude is \(45^\circ\).

\(Choose\;the\;correct\;alternatives\)
\(\;\;(a)\) Both the statements \((i)\) and \((ii)\) are correct.
\(\;\;(b)\) Both the statements \((i)\) and \((ii)\) are incorrect.
\(\;\;(c)\) Statement \((i)\) is correct but statement \((ii)\) is incorrect.
\(\;\;(d)\) Statement \((i)\) is incorrect but statement \((ii)\) is correct.

Solution:\(Option\;(d)\;Statement\;(i)\;is\) \(incorrect\) \(but\) \(Statement\) \((ii)\) \(is\) \(correct.\)


Explanation:

\(\quad Statement\;(i):\)

\(\quad \tan30^\circ=\dfrac{\text{Height of tower}}{\text{Distance from foot of tower}} \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}=\dfrac{h}{100} \)


\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow h=\dfrac{100}{\sqrt{3}} \)


\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow h=\dfrac{100\sqrt{3}}{3}\;m \)

\(\quad\therefore \text{Statement }(i)\text{ is incorrect.} \)


\(\quad Statement\,(ii):\)

\(\qquad \tan\theta=\dfrac{\text{Height of pole}}{\text{Length of shadow}} \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \tan\theta=\dfrac{h}{h}=1 \)

\(\qquad\quad\Rightarrow \tan\theta=\tan45^\circ \)

\(\qquad\qquad \therefore\theta=45^\circ \)

\(\quad\therefore \text{Statement }(ii)\text{ is correct.} \)

NCERT Class 10 Math Chapter 9 – Some Applications of Trigonometry FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Heights and Distances.

What is angle of elevation?

Angle between the horizontal line and the line of sight when looking at an object above the horizontal.

What is angle of depression?

Angle between the horizontal line and the line of sight when looking at an object below the horizontal.

How to solve heights and distances problems?

Draw a right‑angled triangle. Use tan, sin, or cos depending on known sides. Tan is most common for height/distance.

What is the relation between angle of elevation and depression?

They are equal when measured from two points on the same horizontal level (alternate interior angles).

Which trigonometric ratio is most used?

Tangent, because it directly relates the opposite side (height) to the adjacent side (distance).

How to find height using two angles?

Create two right triangles, express the common side in terms of the unknown, equate and solve.

What is the value of √3 used in problems?

√3 ≈ 1.732. Many answers are expressed in terms of √3.

What should I do first in a word problem?

Draw a neat diagram, mark the given and unknown quantities, and decide which trigonometric ratio applies.

Why is the boy's height subtracted in some problems?

Because the angle of elevation is measured from the observer's eye level, not from the ground.

Can I use sine or cosine instead of tangent?

Yes, but you must know the hypotenuse. Tangent is easier when only the legs are involved.
