Coordinate Geometry is the study of geometry using a coordinate system. In Class 10, you will learn to find distances between two points, divide a line segment in a given ratio, and calculate the area of a triangle using coordinates.

This chapter is essential because it connects algebra and geometry, allowing you to solve geometrical problems with equations. Mastering these formulas helps in advanced topics like calculus and analytical geometry.

7. Find the point on the \(x-axis\) which is equidistant from \((2, -5)\) and \((-2, 9)\).

Solution: Let point on x-axis be (x,0). Then distance to (2,-5) = distance to (-2,9).
\(\sqrt{(x-2)^2 + (0+5)^2} = \sqrt{(x+2)^2 + (0-9)^2}\)
Squaring: \((x-2)^2 + 25 = (x+2)^2 + 81\)
\(x^2 -4x +4 +25 = x^2 +4x +4 +81\)
\(-4x +29 = 4x +85 \;\;\Rightarrow -8x = 56 \;\;\Rightarrow x = -7\).
Point is (–7, 0).

8. Find the values of \(y\) for which the distance between the points \(P(2, - 3)\) and \(Q(10, y)\) is \(10\) units.

Solution: PQ = \(\sqrt{(10-2)^2 + (y+3)^2} = \sqrt{64 + (y+3)^2} = 10\)
Squaring: \(64 + (y+3)^2 = 100 \;\;\Rightarrow (y+3)^2 = 36 \;\;\Rightarrow y+3 = \pm 6\)
∴ y = 3 or y = –9.

9. If \(Q(0, 1)\) is equidistant from \(P(5, -3)\) and \(R(x, 6)\), find the values of \(x\). Also find the distances \(QR\) and \(PR\).

Solution: QP = \(\sqrt{(5-0)^2 + (-3-1)^2} = \sqrt{25+16} = \sqrt{41}\).
QR = \(\sqrt{(x-0)^2 + (6-1)^2} = \sqrt{x^2+25}\).
Equidistant → \(\sqrt{x^2+25} = \sqrt{41}\) ⇒ \(x^2+25=41\) ⇒ \(x^2=16\) ⇒ x = ±4.
For x=4: QR = \(\sqrt{41}\), PR = \(\sqrt{(5-4)^2+(-3-6)^2} = \sqrt{1+81} = \sqrt{82}\).
For x=-4: QR = \(\sqrt{41}\), PR = \(\sqrt{(5+4)^2+(-3-6)^2} = \sqrt{81+81} = \sqrt{162} = 9\sqrt{2}\).

10. Find a relation between \(x\) and \(y\) such that the point \((x, y)\) is equidistant from the point \((3, 6)\) and \((-3, 4)\).

Solution: \(\sqrt{(x-3)^2 + (y-6)^2} = \sqrt{(x+3)^2 + (y-4)^2}\)
Squaring: \((x-3)^2 + (y-6)^2 = (x+3)^2 + (y-4)^2\)
\(x^2-6x+9 + y^2-12y+36 = x^2+6x+9 + y^2-8y+16\)
Cancel \(x^2, y^2, 9\): \(-6x -12y +36 = 6x -8y +16\)
Bring terms: \(-6x-6x -12y+8y = 16-36\) ⇒ \(-12x -4y = -20\)
Divide by –4: \(3x + y = 5\). This is the required relation.

Exercise 7.2

1. Find the coordinates of the point which divides the join of \((-1, 7)\) and \((4, -3)\) in the ratio \(2 : 3\).

Solution: Using section formula: \(x = \frac{2(4)+3(-1)}{2+3} = \frac{8-3}{5} = 1\), \(y = \frac{2(-3)+3(7)}{5} = \frac{-6+21}{5} = 3\). Point = (1,3).

2. Find the coordinates of the points of trisection of the line segment joining \((4, -1)\) and \((-2, -3)\).

Solution: Trisection points divide segment in ratios 1:2 and 2:1.
For 1:2: \(x = \frac{1(-2)+2(4)}{3} = \frac{-2+8}{3}=2\), \(y = \frac{1(-3)+2(-1)}{3} = \frac{-3-2}{3} = -\frac{5}{3}\). Point P(2, -5/3).
For 2:1: \(x = \frac{2(-2)+1(4)}{3} = \frac{-4+4}{3}=0\), \(y = \frac{2(-3)+1(-1)}{3} = \frac{-6-1}{3} = -\frac{7}{3}\). Point Q(0, -7/3).

3. To conduct Sports Day activities, in your rectangular shaped school ground \(ABCD\), lines have been drawn with chalk powder at a distance of \(1 m\) each. \(100\) flower pots have been placed at a distance of \(1m\) from each other along \(AD\), as shown in \(Fig. 7.14\). Niharika runs \(\dfrac{1}{4}th\) the distance \(AD\) on the \(2^{nd}\) line and posts a green flag. Padma runs \(\dfrac{1}{5}th\) the distance \(AD\) on the \(eighth\) line and posts a red flag. What is the distance between both the flags? If Rashmi has to post a blue flag exactly halfway between the line segment joining the two flags, where should she post her flag?

Solution: Niharika: 2nd line, 1/4 of AD = coordinates (2, 100/4) = (2,25). Preet: 8th line, 1/5 of AD = (8, 100/5) = (8,20).
Distance = \(\sqrt{(8-2)^2+(20-25)^2} = \sqrt{36+25} = \sqrt{61}\) m.
Midpoint = \(\left(\frac{2+8}{2}, \frac{25+20}{2}\right) = (5, 22.5)\).

4. Find the ratio in which the line segment joining the points \((- 3, 10)\) and \((6, - 8)\) is divided by \((- 1, 6)\).

Solution: Let ratio k:1. Then \(-1 = \frac{k(6)+1(-3)}{k+1}\) → \(-k-1 = 6k-3\) ⇒ \(7k = 2\) ⇒ k = 2/7. Ratio = 2:7.
Check y: \(\frac{k(-8)+1(10)}{k+1} = \frac{(2/7)(-8)+10}{2/7+1} = \frac{-16/7+10}{9/7} = \frac{54/7}{9/7} = 6\). Correct.

5. Find the ratio in which the line segment joining \(A(1, - 5)\) and \(B(- 4, 5)\) is divided by the \(x-axis\). Also find the coordinates of the point of division.

Solution: Let ratio k:1. Point on x-axis has y=0. \(0 = \frac{k(5)+1(-5)}{k+1}\) → \(0 = 5k-5\) → k=1. Ratio = 1:1.
Coordinates: \(x = \frac{1(-4)+1(1)}{2} = \frac{-3}{2}\), so point = (-1.5, 0).

6. If \((1, 2)\), \((4, y)\), \((x, 6)\) and \((3, 5)\) are the vertices of a parallelogram taken in order, find \(x\) and \(y\).

Solution: In a parallelogram, diagonals bisect. Midpoint of diagonal between (1,2) and (x,6) equals midpoint of (4,y) and (3,5).
\(\left(\frac{1+x}{2}, \frac{2+6}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{4+3}{2}, \frac{y+5}{2}\right)\)
x-coordinate: \(1+x = 7\) ⇒ x=6.
y-coordinate: \(8 = y+5\) ⇒ y=3.

7. Find the coordinates of a point \(A\), where \(AB\) is the diameter of a circle whose centre is \((2, - 3)\) and \(B\) is \((1, 4)\).

Solution: Centre is midpoint of A and B. Let A(x,y).
\(\frac{x+1}{2}=2\) ⇒ x+1=4 ⇒ x=3.
\(\frac{y+4}{2}=-3\) ⇒ y+4=-6 ⇒ y=-10. So A(3, –10).

8. If \(A\) and \(B\) are \((- 2, - 2)\) and \((2, - 4)\), respectively, find the coordinates of \(P\) such that \(AP\) \(=\) \(\dfrac{3}{7}\)\(AB\) and \(P\) lies on the line segment \(AB\).

Solution: AP:PB = 3:4 (since AP = 3/7 AB, PB = AB - AP = 4/7 AB).
So P divides AB in ratio 3:4.
\(x = \frac{3(2)+4(-2)}{7} = \frac{6-8}{7} = -\frac{2}{7}\), \(y = \frac{3(-4)+4(-2)}{7} = \frac{-12-8}{7} = -\frac{20}{7}\). P = (-2/7, -20/7).

9. Find the coordinates of the points which divide the line segment joining \(A(- 2, 2)\) and \(B(2, 8)\) into four equal parts.

Solution: Points dividing into 4 equal parts: they divide AB in ratios 1:3, 2:2 (midpoint), 3:1.
Midpoint (1:1): \(\left(\frac{-2+2}{2}, \frac{2+8}{2}\right) = (0,5)\).
Point dividing 1:3 (closer to A): \(x = \frac{1(2)+3(-2)}{4} = \frac{2-6}{4} = -1\), \(y = \frac{1(8)+3(2)}{4} = \frac{8+6}{4} = 3.5\). (-1, 3.5).
Point dividing 3:1: \(x = \frac{3(2)+1(-2)}{4} = \frac{6-2}{4}=1\), \(y = \frac{3(8)+1(2)}{4} = \frac{24+2}{4}=6.5\). (1, 6.5).

NCERT Class 10 Math Chapter 7 – Coordinate Geometry FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Coordinate Geometry.

What is the distance formula?

The distance between two points \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) is \(\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}\).

What is the section formula?

Coordinates of a point dividing the line segment joining \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) internally in the ratio \(m:n\) are \(\left(\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n}, \frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n}\right)\).

How to find the midpoint?

Midpoint = \(\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)\). It is a special case of section formula when \(m=n\).

How to check if three points are collinear?

Either show that the sum of two distances equals the third (distance formula) or that the area of the triangle formed is zero.

What is the area of triangle formula in coordinate geometry?

Area = \(\frac{1}{2} | x_1(y_2-y_3) + x_2(y_3-y_1) + x_3(y_1-y_2) |\).

How to find the distance of a point from the axes?

Distance from x-axis = \(|y|\); distance from y-axis = \(|x|\).

Can we use coordinate geometry to classify quadrilaterals?

Yes, find side lengths and diagonals using distance formula; check properties (e.g., opposite sides equal for parallelogram, all sides equal for rhombus).

What is the centroid of a triangle?

The centroid is the intersection of medians; coordinates = \(\left(\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3}, \frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3}\right)\).

How to prove a right triangle using coordinates?

Show that the square of the longest side equals the sum of squares of the other two (Pythagoras converse).

What is the difference between distance formula and section formula?

Distance formula finds the length between two points; section formula finds the coordinates of a point dividing the segment in a given ratio.
