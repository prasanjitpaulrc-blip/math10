Arithmetic Progressions (AP) are sequences of numbers in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant. This chapter covers the identification of APs, finding the nth term, and computing the sum of the first n terms. It builds a strong foundation for understanding sequences and series, which are essential for higher mathematics and competitive exams.

From real‑life applications like calculating savings growth to solving complex algebraic problems, AP is one of the most practical and scoring chapters in Class 10. Mastering the formulas \(a_n = a + (n-1)d\) and \(S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]\) will make you confident in tackling a wide variety of problems.

Exercise 5.1

1. In which of the following situations, does the list of numbers involved make an arithmetic progression, and why?

 \((i)\) The taxi fare after each km when the fare is ₹\(15\) for the first km and ₹\(8\) for each additional km.
 \((ii)\) The amount of air present in a cylinder when a vacuum pump removes \(\frac{1}{4}\) of the air remaining in the cylinder at a time.
 \((iii)\) The cost of digging a well after every metre of digging, when it costs ₹\(150\) for the first metre and rises by ₹\(50\) for each subsequent metre.
 \((iv)\) The amount of money in the account every year, when ₹\(10000\) is deposited at compound interest at \(8\) % per annum.

Solution:

\(\quad(i)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Let \(a_n\) be the taxi fare for the first \(n\) km.

\(\quad\)Then,

\(\qquad\quad a_1=a=15\)

\(\qquad\quad a_2=15+8=23\)

\(\qquad\quad a_3=23+8=31\)

\(\quad\)\(So, \;the \;list \;is\)

\(\qquad\quad 15,\;23,\;31,\;\ldots\)

\(\qquad\)\(Here\),

\(\quad\quad a_2-a_1=a_3-a_2=\cdots=8\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;given \;list \;forms\) \(an\) \(A.P.\)



\(\quad(ii)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Let the first term be \(x\) units.

\(\quad\)Then,

\(\qquad\quad a_1=a=x\)

\(\qquad\quad a_2=x-\dfrac14x=\dfrac34x\)

\(\qquad a_3=\dfrac34x-\dfrac14\left(\dfrac34x\right)=\dfrac9{16}x\)

\(\quad\;\; a_4=\dfrac9{16}x-\dfrac14\left(\dfrac9{16}x\right)=\)\(\dfrac{27}{64}x\)

\(\quad\)\(So, \;the \;list \;is\)

\(\qquad\quad x,\;\dfrac34x,\;\dfrac9{16}x,\;\dfrac{27}{64}x,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)\(Here\),

\(\qquad\quad a_2-a_1\ne a_3-a_2\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;given \;list \;does \;not\) \(form\) \(an\) \(A.P\).



\(\quad(iii)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)First term \(a=150\).

\(\quad\)Common difference for every subsequent metre is \(50\).

\(\quad\)Then,

\(\qquad a_1=a=150\)

\(\qquad a_2=a+d=150+50=\)\(200\)

\(\; a_3=a+2d=150+2(50)=\)\(250\)

\(\; a_4=a+3d=150+3(50)=\)\(300\)

\(\quad\)\(Here\),

\(\quad\qquad a_2-a_1=a_3-a_2=50\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;given \;list \;forms\) \(an\) \(A.P\).



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Let \(a_n\) be the amount of money at the end of the \(n^{\text{th}}\) year.

\(\quad\)Then,

\(\quad a_1=a=10000\)

\( a_2=10000+\dfrac8{100}\times10000=\)\(10800\)


\( a_3=10800+\dfrac8{100}\times10800=\)\(11664\)


\( a_4=11664+\dfrac8{100}\times11664=\)\(12597.12\)

\(So, \;the \;list \;is\)

\( 10000,\;10800,\;11664,\;12597.12,\;\ldots\)

\(\qquad\)\(Here\),

\(\qquad\quad a_2-a_1\ne a_3-a_2\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;given \;list \;does \;not\) \(form\) \(an\) \(A.P\).

2. Write first four terms of the \(AP\), when the first term \(a\) and the common difference \(d\) are given as follows:
  \((i)\) \(a = 10\), \(d = 10\)
  \((ii)\) \(a = -2\), \(d = 0\)
 \((iii)\) \(a = 4\), \(d = -3\)
 \((iv)\) \(a = -1\), \(d = \frac{1}{2}\)
 \((v)\) \(a = -1.25\), \(d = -0.25\)

Solution:

\(\quad(i)\)

\(\quad\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad a=10,\;d=10\)

\(\quad\)The first four terms are:

\(\qquad\quad a=10\)

\(\qquad\quad a+d=10+10=20\)

\(\qquad\quad a+2d=10+2(10)=30\)

\(\qquad\quad a+3d=10+3(10)=40\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;first \;four \;terms \;are\) \(10,\) \(20,\) \(30,\) \(40.\)



\(\quad(ii)\)

\(\quad\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad a=-2,\;d=0\)

\(\quad\)The first four terms are:

\(\qquad\quad a=-2\)

\(\qquad\quad a+d=-2+0=-2\)

\(\qquad\quad a+2d=-2+2(0)=-2\)

\(\qquad\quad a+3d=-2+3(0)=-2\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;first \;four \;terms \;are\) \(-2,\) \(-2,\) \(-2,\) \(-2.\)



\(\quad(iii)\)

\(\quad\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad a=4,\;d=-3\)

\(\quad\)The first four terms are:

\(\qquad\quad a=4\)

\(\qquad\quad a+d=4+(-3)=1\)

\(\qquad\quad a+2d=4+2(-3)=-2\)

\(\qquad\quad a+3d=4+3(-3)=-5\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;first \;four \;terms \;are\) \(4,\) \(1,\) \(-2,\) \(-5.\)



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\quad\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad a=-1,\;d=\dfrac12\)

\(\quad\)The first four terms are:

\(\qquad\quad a=-1\)

\(\qquad\quad a+d=-1+\dfrac12=-\dfrac12\)

\(\quad\quad a+2d=-1+2\left(\dfrac12\right)=0\)

\(\quad\quad a+3d=-1+3\left(\dfrac12\right)=\dfrac12\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;first \;four \;terms \;are\) \(-1,\) \(-\dfrac12,\) \(0,\) \(\dfrac12.\)



\(\quad(v)\)

\(\quad\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad a=-1.25,\;d=-0.25\)

\(\quad\)The first four terms are:

\(\qquad\quad a=-1.25\)

\(\;\; a+d=-1.25+(-0.25)=-1.50\)
\(\;\; a+2d=-1.25+2(-0.25)=-1.75\)
\(\;\; a+3d=-1.25+3(-0.25)=-2.00\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;first \;four \;terms \;are\) \(-1.25,\) \(-1.50,\) \(-1.75,\) \(-2.00.\)

3. For the following \(APs\), write the first term and the common difference:
 \((i)\) \(3, 1, -1, -3, \dots\)
 \((ii)\) \(-5, -1, 3, 7, \dots\)
 \((iii)\) \(\frac{1}{3}, \frac{5}{3}, \frac{9}{3}, \frac{13}{3}, \dots\)
 \((iv)\) \(0.6, 1.7, 2.8, 3.9, \dots\)

Solution:

\(\quad(i)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Given A.P.:

\(\qquad\quad\;\; 3,\;1,\;-1,\;-3,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)The first term is

\(\qquad\quad a=a_1=3\)

\(\quad\)The common difference is

\(\qquad\quad d=a_2-a_1\)

\(\qquad\qquad =1-3\)

\(\qquad\qquad =-2\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(a=3,\;d=-2.\)



\(\quad(ii)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Given A.P.:

\(\qquad\quad\;\; -5,\;-1,\;3,\;7,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)The first term is

\(\qquad\quad a=a_1=-5\)

\(\quad\)The common difference is

\(\qquad\quad d=a_2-a_1\)

\(\qquad\qquad =-1-(-5)\)

\(\qquad\qquad =4\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(a=-5,\;d=4.\)



\(\quad(iii)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Given A.P.:

\(\qquad\quad\;\; \dfrac13,\;\dfrac53,\;\dfrac93,\;\dfrac{13}3,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)The first term is

\(\qquad\quad a=a_1=\dfrac13\)

\(\quad\)The common difference is

\(\qquad\quad d=a_2-a_1\)


\(\qquad\qquad =\dfrac53-\dfrac13\)


\(\qquad\qquad =\dfrac43\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(a=\dfrac13,\;d=\dfrac43.\)



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Given A.P.:

\(\qquad\quad\;\; 0.6,\;1.7,\;2.8,\;3.9,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)The first term is

\(\qquad\quad a=a_1=0.6\)

\(\quad\)The common difference is

\(\qquad\quad d=a_2-a_1\)

\(\qquad\qquad =1.7-0.6\)

\(\qquad\qquad =1.1\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(a=0.6,\;d=1.1.\)

Solution:

\(\quad(i)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad\;\; 2,\;4,\;8,\;16,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)Let the first term be \(a_1\) and the common difference be \(d\).

\(\qquad\quad a_1=2\)

\(\qquad\quad d=a_2-a_1\)

\(\qquad\qquad =4-2=2\)

\(\qquad\quad d=a_3-a_2\)

\(\qquad\qquad =8-4=4\)

\(\qquad\because\)\(\quad a_2-a_1 \ne a_3-a_2\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;given \;sequence \;is \;not\) \(an\) \(A.P\).



\(\quad(ii)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad\;\; 2,\;\dfrac52,\;3,\;\dfrac72,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)Let the first term be \(a_1\) and the common difference be \(d\).

\(\qquad\quad a_1=2\)

\(\qquad\quad d=a_2-a_1\)

\(\qquad\qquad =\dfrac52-2=\dfrac12\)


\(\qquad\quad d=a_3-a_2\)

\(\qquad\qquad =3-\dfrac52=\dfrac12\)


\(\qquad\quad d=a_4-a_3\)

\(\qquad\qquad =\dfrac72-3=\dfrac12\)


\(\quad\because\)\(\; a_2-a_1 = a_3-a_2= a_4-a_3\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The\;given\;sequence\) \(is\) \(an\) \(A.P.\) \(with\) \(d=\dfrac12.\)


\(\quad\)\(Next \;three \;terms \;are\) \(4,\;\dfrac92,\;5.\)



\(\quad(iii)\)

\(\quad\;\;\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad -1.2,\;-3.2,\;-5.2,\;-7.2,\ldots\)

\(\qquad\qquad =4-2=2\)

There are three sections of each class. How many trees will be planted by the students?

\(\quad\)The number of trees planted by one section of each class forms the AP

\(\qquad\quad 1,\;2,\;3,\;\ldots,\;12\)

\(\quad\)Here,

\(\qquad a=1,\quad l=12,\quad n=12\)

\(\quad\)\(We \;know \;that,\)

\(\qquad\quad S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}(a+l)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow S_{12}=\dfrac{12}{2}(1+12)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow S_{12}=6\times13\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow S_{12}=78\)

19. \(200\) logs are stacked in the following manner: \(20\) logs in the bottom row, \(19\) in the next row, \(18\) in the row next to it and so on \((see Fig. 5.5)\). In how many rows are the \(200\) logs and how many logs are in the top row?

Solution:

\(\quad\)The number of logs in each row forms the \(AP\)

\(\qquad\quad 20,\;19,\;18,\;17,\;\ldots\)

\(\quad\)\(Here,\)

\(\qquad a=20,\quad d=-1,\quad S_n=200\)

\(\quad\)\(We \;know \;that,\)

\(\qquad\quad S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]\)

\(\;\;\Rightarrow 200=\dfrac{n}{2}[2(20)+(n-1)(-1)]\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 200=\dfrac{n}{2}(40-n+1)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 400=n(41-n)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 400=41n-n^2\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow n^2-41n+400=0\)

\(\quad\;\;\Rightarrow n^2-16n-25n+400=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow (n-16)(n-25)=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow n=16\quad \text{or}\quad 25\)

\(\quad\)\(Since \;the \;top \;row \;must \;contain\) \(at\) \(least\) \(one\) \(log,\)

\(\qquad\quad a_n=a+(n-1)d\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow a_{25}=20+24(-1)=-4\)

\(\qquad\)\(This \;is \;not \;possible.\)

\(\qquad\therefore n=16\)

\(\quad\)\(Now,\)

\(\qquad\quad a_{16}=20+15(-1)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow a_{16}=5\)

\(\;\;\therefore\) \(There \;are\) \(16\) \(rows\) \(and\) \(the\) \(top\) \(row\) \(contains\) \(5\) \(logs.\)

23. Which of the following is correct for the \(AP\) \(5, 9, 13, \ldots, 185?\)

\((i)\) \(a_n = 4 + 4n\)  \((ii)\) \(a_1 + a_{10} = 30\)
\((iii)\) \(S_n = \frac{n}{2}\times190\)      \((iv)\) \(n = 43\)

\((A)\) \((i)\), \((iii)\)
\((B)\) \((ii)\), \((iii)\)
\((C)\) \((i)\), \((ii)\), \((iii)\)
\((D)\) \((i)\), \((ii)\), \((iii)\), \((iv)\)

Solution:\((A)\) \((i)\),\(\;(iii)\) are correct.



Explanation:

\(\quad\)Given \(AP:\;5,\;9,\;13,\;\ldots,\;185\)

\(\quad\)Here,

\(\qquad a=5,\quad d=4,\quad l=185\)

\(\qquad\quad a_n=a+(n-1)d\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow a_n=5+(n-1)\times4\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow a_n=5+4n-4\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow a_n=4+4n\)

\(\qquad\therefore\) Statement \((i)\) is true.

24. \(Assertion (A)\): The sum of first \(10\) terms of the sequence \(11, 13, 15, 17, \ldots\) is \(200\).
\(Reason (R)\): The sum of first \(n\) odd natural numbers is \(n^2\).

(A) \((A)\) and \((R)\) both are true and \((R)\) is the correct explanation for \((A)\)
(B) \((A)\) and \((R)\) both are true but \((R)\) is not the correct explanation for \((A)\)
(C) \((A)\) is true but \((R)\) is false
(D) \((A)\) is false but \((R)\) is true

Solution:\((B)\) is the correct option.



Explanation:

\(\quad\)For the given sequence,

\(\qquad a=11,\quad d=2,\quad n=10\)

\(\quad\)Using the formula,

\(\qquad\quad S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow S_{10}=\dfrac{10}{2}[2(11)+9(2)]\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow S_{10}=5(22+18)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow S_{10}=5\times40\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow S_{10}=200\)

\(\quad\therefore\) Assertion (A) is true.


\(\quad\)Also, the sum of the first \(n\) odd natural numbers is

\(\quad\; 1+3+5+\cdots+(2n-1)=n^2\)

\(\quad\therefore\) Reason (R) is true.


\(\quad\)However, the assertion is proved using the sum formula of an AP,

\(\qquad\; S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d],\)

\(\quad\)not by using the fact that the sum of the first \(n\) odd natural numbers is \(n^2\).

\(\quad\therefore\) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).

25. If the sum of the first \(n\) odd numbers is \(S_1\) and the sum of the first \(n\) natural numbers is \(S_2\), then the value of \(\frac{S_1}{S_2}\) is:

\((A)\) \(\frac{2n}{n+1}\)   \((B)\) \(\frac{n+1}{2n}\)   \((C)\) \(\frac{n}{n+1}\)   \((D)\) \(\frac{n+1}{n}\)

\(\qquad\quad S_2=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\)

26. A fruit seller brings a carton of apples which contains \(240\) apples. He arranges the apples such that in the first row \(6\) apples, in the second row \(10\) apples, in the third row \(14\) apples and so on.

\((i)\) Find the total number of rows of apples.
\((ii)\) Find the difference in number of apples placed in \(7^{th}\) and \(3^{rd}\) row.
\((iii)\) Find the number of apples in the last \(3^{rd}\) row.
\((iv)\) Check if there is any row containing \(32\) apples.

Solution:

\(\quad\)Given,

\(\qquad\quad a=6,\quad d=4,\quad S_n=240\)


\(\quad(i)\)

\(\quad\)\(we\;know\;that,\)

\(\qquad\quad S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 240=\dfrac{n}{2}[2(6)+(n-1)4]\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 480=n[12+4n-4]\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 480=n(4n+8)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 480=4n(n+2)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 120=n(n+2)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 120=n^2+2n\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow n^2+2n-120=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow n^2-10n+12n-120=0\)

\(\quad\;\Rightarrow n(n-10)+12(n-10)=0\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow (n-10)(n+12)=0\)

\(\;\Rightarrow n=10\;or\;n=-12(not \;possible)\)

\(\;\;\therefore\) \(The \;total \;number \;of \;rows \;is\) \(10\).



\(\quad(ii)\)

\(\quad\)Using,

\(\qquad\quad a_n=a+(n-1)d\)

\(\qquad\quad a_7=6+(7-1)\times4=30\)

\(\qquad\quad a_3=6+(3-1)\times4=14\)

\(\qquad Difference=30-14=16\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;required \;difference \;is\) \(16\).



\(\quad(iii)\)

\(\quad\)Since there are \(10\) rows, the last \(3^{rd}\) row is the

\(\qquad\quad (10-2)=8^{th}\text{ row}\)

\(\qquad\quad a_8=6+(8-1)\times4\)

\(\qquad\quad =6+28=34\)

\(\quad\therefore\) \(The \;number \;of \;apples\; in\) \(the\) \(last\) \(3^{rd}\) \(row\) \(is\) \(34\).



\(\quad(iv)\)

\(\quad\)Let the row containing \(32\) apples be the \(n^{th}\) row.

\(\qquad\quad a_n=32\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 32=6+(n-1)4\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow 26=4(n-1)\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow n-1=\dfrac{13}{2}\)

\(\qquad\Rightarrow n=\dfrac{15}{2}=7.5\)

\(\quad\)\(Since \;the \;row \;number \;is \;not\) \(a\) \(whole\) \(number,\) \(no\) \(such\) \(row\) \(exists\).

\(\quad\therefore\) \(There \;is \;no \;row \;containing\) \(32\) \(apples.\)

NCERT Class 10 Math Chapter 5 - Arithmetic Progressions FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Arithmetic Progressions.

What is an Arithmetic Progression (AP)?

A sequence of numbers in which the difference between any two consecutive terms is constant. Example: 3, 7, 11, 15, … (d=4).

What is the nth term formula?

\(a_n = a + (n-1)d\), where a is first term, d is common difference.

What is the sum of first n terms?

\(S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] = \frac{n}{2}[a + l]\), where l is the last term.

How to find the common difference?

\(d = a_{k} - a_{k-1}\) for any k>1. For an AP, it is constant.

Can an AP have zero common difference?

Yes, then all terms are equal (constant sequence). Example: 5,5,5,…

How to find the number of terms in an AP?

Use \(a_n = a + (n-1)d\) and solve for n if the last term is known.

What is the sum of first n natural numbers?

\(1+2+3+\dots+n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}\).

Can the common difference be negative?

Yes, then the terms decrease. Example: 10, 7, 4, 1, … (d=-3).

What is the relation between a, d, and the last term l?

\(l = a + (n-1)d\). From this we can find any unknown.

How to solve word problems in AP?

Identify the first term, common difference, and the required number of terms from the statement, then apply the appropriate formula.
