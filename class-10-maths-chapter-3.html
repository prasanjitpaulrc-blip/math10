2. On compairing the ratios \(\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\), \(\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\), and \(\dfrac{c_1}{c_2}\), find out whether the lines representing the following pairs of linear equations intersect at a point, are parallel or coincident:

 \(\quad(i)\)  \( 5x - 4y + 8=0\)
\(\quad\quad\)and \(7x + 6y - 9 = 0\)

 \(\quad(ii)\) \(9x + 3y + 12 = 0\)
\(\quad\quad\)and \(18x + 6y + 24 = 0\)

 \(\quad(iii)\) \(6x - 3y +10 = 0\)
\(\quad\quad\) and  \(2x - y + 9 = 0\)

Solution:
\(\quad (i) Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 5x-4y+8=0\),
\(\qquad\;\; and \;\; 7x+6y-9=0\)

\( Here,\)

\(\quad\;\; a_1=5,\quad b_1=-4\),\(\quad c_1=8\),

\(\quad\; a_2=7,\quad b_2=6,\quad c_2=-9\)
\(On \;comparing \;the \;ratios, \;we\) \(have\)

\(\quad\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{5}{7},\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{-4}{6}=-\dfrac{2}{3}\)

\(\qquad Since \; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\ne\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\),

\(\;So, \;the\; two \;lines\) \(representing\) \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(intersect\) \(at\) \(one\) \(point\).


\(\quad (ii)\; Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 9x+3y+12=0\),
\(\qquad\;\; and \;\; 18x+6y+24=0\)

\(Here,\)

\(\qquad a_1=9,\quad b_1=3,\quad c_1=12,\)

\(\quad\;\; a_2=18,\quad b_2=6,\quad c_2=24\)
\(On\; comparing\; the\; ratios,\; we\) \(have\)

\(\;\;\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{9}{18}=\dfrac12\),\(\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{3}{6}=\dfrac12\),
\(\qquad \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}=\dfrac{12}{24}=\dfrac12\)

\(\qquad Since\; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{c_1}{c_2},\)

\(\;So,\; the\; two\; lines\) \(representing\) \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(are\) \(coincident\).


\(\quad (iii)\; Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 6x-3y+10=0\),
\(\qquad\quad\; and \;\; 2x-y+9=0\)

\(Here,\)

\(\qquad a_1=6,\quad b_1=-3,\)\(\; c_1=10,\)

\(\qquad\; a_2=2,\quad b_2=-1,\quad c_2=9\)
\(On\; comparing\; the\; ratios,\; we\) \(have\)

\(\;\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{6}{2}=3,\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{-3}{-1}=3\),
\(\qquad \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}=\dfrac{10}{9}\)

\(\qquad Since\; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\ne\dfrac{c_1}{c_2},\)

\(\;So,\; the\; two\; lines\) \(representing\) \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(are\) \(parallel\).

3. On compairing the ratios \(\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\), \(\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\), and \(\dfrac{c_1}{c_2}\), find out whether the following pairs of linear equations are coincident, or inconsistent:

 \(\quad(i)\) \(3x + 2y = 5\);
\(\quad\quad\) \(2x - 3y = 7\)

 \(\quad(ii)\) \(2x - 3y = 8\);
\(\quad\quad\) \(4x - 6y = 9\)

 \(\quad(iii)\) \(\dfrac{3}{2}x + \dfrac{5}{3}y = 7\);
\(\quad\quad\) \(9x - 10y = 14 \)

 \(\quad(iv)\) \( 5x - 3y = 11\);
\(\quad\quad\) \(-10x + 16y = -22 \)

 \(\quad(v)\) \(\dfrac{4}{3}x + 2y = 8\);
\(\quad\quad\) \(2x + 3y = 12 \)

Solution:
\(\quad (i)\; Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 3x+2y=5,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow 3x+2y-5=0,\)
\(\qquad\; \;and\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 2x-3y=7\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow2x-3y-7=0\)

\(Here,\)

\(\qquad a_1=3,\quad b_1=2,\; c_1=-5,\)

\(\quad\;\; a_2=2,\quad b_2=-3,\quad c_2=-7\)
\(On\; comparing\; the\; ratios,\; we\) \(have\)

\(\quad\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac32,\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{2}{-3}=-\dfrac23\)

\(\qquad Since\; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\ne\dfrac{b_1}{b_2},\)

\(\;So\), \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(are\) \(consistent\) .


\(\quad (ii)\; Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 2x-3y=8,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow 2x-3y-8=0,\)
\(\qquad\; \;and\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 4x-6y=9\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow 4x-6y-9=0\)

\(Here,\)

\(\qquad a_1=2,\quad b_1=-3,\; c_1=-8,\)

\(\quad\;\; a_2=4,\quad b_2=-6,\quad c_2=-9\)
\(On\; comparing\; the\; ratios,\; we\) \(have\)

\(\quad\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac24=\dfrac12,\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{-3}{-6}=\dfrac12\),
\(\qquad \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}=\dfrac{-8}{-9}=\dfrac89\)

\(\qquad Since\; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}\ne\dfrac{c_1}{c_2},\)

\(\;So\), \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(are\) \(inconsistent\) .


\(\quad (iii)\; Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad \dfrac32x+\dfrac53y=7,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow \dfrac32x+\dfrac53y-7=0,\)
\(\qquad\; \;and\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 9x-10y=14\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow 9x-10y-14=0\)

\(Here,\)

\(\qquad a_1=\dfrac32,\quad b_1=\dfrac53,\; c_1=-7,\)

\(\quad\; a_2=9,\quad b_2=-10,\; c_2=-14\)
\(On\; comparing\; the\; ratios,\; we\) \(have\)

\(\quad\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{\frac32}{9}=\dfrac16,\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{\dfrac53}{-10}=\dfrac{-1}{6}\)

\(\qquad Since\; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\ne\dfrac{b_1}{b_2},\)

\(\;So\), \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(are\) \(consistent\) .


\(\quad (iv)\; Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 5x-3y=11,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow 5x-3y-11=0,\)
\(\qquad\; \;and\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad -10x+16y=-22\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow -10x+16y+22=0\)

\(Here,\)

\(\quad\;\; a_1=5,\quad b_1=-3,\; c_1=-11,\)

\(\quad\;\; a_2=-10,\quad b_2=16,\; c_2=22\)
\(On\; comparing\; the\; ratios,\; we\) \(have\)

\(\quad\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=-\dfrac12,\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=-\dfrac{3}{16}\)

\(\qquad Since\; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\ne\dfrac{b_1}{b_2},\)

\(\;So\), \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(are\) \(consistent\) .


\(\quad (v)\; Given,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\qquad \dfrac43x+2y=8,\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad \dfrac43x+2y-8=0,\)
\(\qquad\; \;and\)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 2x+3y-12=0\)
\(\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow 2x+3y-12=0\)

\(Here,\)

\(\qquad\; a_1=\dfrac43,\quad b_1=2,\; c_1=-8,\)
\(\qquad a_2=2,\quad b_2=3,\quad c_2=-12\)
\(On\; comparing\; the\; ratios,\; we\) \(have\)

\(\quad\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac23,\quad \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac23,\quad \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}=\dfrac23\)

\(\qquad Since\; \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{c_1}{c_2},\)

\(\;So\), \(the\) \(pair\) \(of\) \(linear\) \(equations\) \(are\) \(coincident\).

8. For what value of \(P\) does the pair of equations given below have a unique solution?

\(\quad 4x + Py + 8 = 0\)
\(\quad 2x + 2y + 2 = 0 \)

\((a)\) When \(P = 4\)   \((b)\) when \(P \neq 4\)
  \((c)\) \(P = -4\)   \(\quad(d)\) \(P \neq -4\)

Solution:\((b)\) \(P \neq4\) is the correct option.

Explanation:
\( \quad Given, \)
\(\qquad\qquad\quad 4x+Py+8=0 \)
\(\qquad\; and\quad 2x+2y+2=0 \)
For a pair of linear equations to have a unique solution, \( \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}\ne\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}. \)
\(\quad\)Here, \( a_1=4,\quad b_1=P\),
\(\quad a_2=2,\quad b_2=2. \)
Therefore, \( \dfrac{a_1}{a_2}=\dfrac{4}{2}=2, \) \( \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}=\dfrac{P}{2}. \)

For a unique solution, \( 2\ne\dfrac{P}{2} \) \( \Rightarrow P\ne4. \)
Hence, the required value is \(P\ne4\)

10. In the folowing question a statement of assertion \((A)\) is followed by a statement of reason \((R)\).

  \( Assertion \) \((A)\): The equation \( 2x - 3y = 0 \) has an infinite number of solutions.

  \( Reason \) \((R)\): A linear equation in two variables represents a straight line on the coordinate plane.

  \(Choose \; the \; correct \; alternative: \)
  \((a)\) Both assertion \((A)\) and reason \((R)\) are true and \((R)\) is the correct explanation of assertion \((A)\).
  \((b)\) Both assertion \((A)\) and reason \((R)\) are true but \((R)\) is not the correct explanation of assertion \((A)\).
  \((c)\) Assertion \((A)\) is true but reason \((R)\) is false.
  \((d)\) Assertion \((A)\) is false but reason \((R)\) is true.

Solution:Option \((a)\) is correct.


Explanation:

NCERT Class 10 Math Chapter 3 – Pair of Linear Equations FAQs

Clear conceptual answers to the most common queries from Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables.

What is a pair of linear equations in two variables?

Two linear equations of the form \( a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0 \) and \( a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0 \), where \( a_1,b_1,c_1,a_2,b_2,c_2 \) are real numbers.

What does it mean if a system is consistent?

It means the system has at least one solution. Graphically, lines intersect or coincide.

How to identify inconsistency from coefficients?

When \( \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2} \). The lines are parallel and distinct.

What is the substitution method?

Express one variable in terms of the other from one equation and substitute into the other equation, then solve.

What is the elimination method?

Make coefficients of one variable equal, then add or subtract to eliminate that variable and solve.

What is the cross‑multiplication method?

For equations \( a_1x+b_1y+c_1=0, a_2x+b_2y+c_2=0 \), \( \frac{x}{b_1c_2-b_2c_1} = \frac{y}{c_1a_2-c_2a_1} = \frac{1}{a_1b_2-a_2b_1} \).

When do two lines have infinitely many solutions?

When \( \frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2} \); the lines coincide.

How to solve equations reducible to linear form?

Substitute new variables for complicated expressions (e.g., \( 1/x \) or \( \frac{1}{x+y} \)), solve the linear system, then back‑substitute.

Can a pair of linear equations have no solution?

Yes, if the lines are parallel (inconsistent system). There is no common point.

What is the graphical representation of a linear equation?

It is a straight line. The solution of a pair of equations is the point of intersection of their lines.
